# Giorgio Morandi (1890-1964)

Deep study, 2026-10-04. Hop-only session, queue exhausted. Named first among
next doorways by the 2026-10-04 Piero della Francesca study ("the remaining
unstudied doorway, the restraint lineage"). The quality-first choice: after the
restraint run (Feldman, Satie, Cage, Byars, Reinhardt, Ryman, Flavin) and the
measured-space studies (Piero, de Chirico, Guston), the master of the interval,
the fixed cast, and the narrowing field. Piero measured space with perspective;
Morandi measured it with the gaps between bottles.

## The technique, as found

The working method, from the Tate pack and the dossier: a fixed cast of
ordinary vessels, physically modified to kill accident (bottle surfaces painted
matte, transparent glass filled with pigment, labels removed, faces removed
from clocks, dust deliberately left to settle; he "was not interested in
optical effects like highlights, but wanted to explore light, colour and
volume"). Three purpose-built tables, including one cut so the back was wider
than the front. Positions notated in pencil on placement sheets, shadows
sometimes drawn in, the marks photographed by Tacita Dean looking like chaos
he could read back exactly; chalk marks for his own feet at the easel so a
viewpoint could be revisited. Weeks of looking, then fast painting: "It takes
me weeks to make up my mind which group of bottles will go well with a
particular coloured tablecloth. Then it takes me weeks of thinking about the
bottles themselves, and yet often I still go wrong with the spaces." Own
pigments, own canvases, palette wiped clean every evening ("nothing was left
to chance"). Serial practice: the four 1952 yellow-cloth paintings (same
vessels, four configurations), the 1956 block series with rich yellow, salmon
and cream rectangles near Minimalist geometry. Titles always conventional:
Still Life plus the year, nothing else. Late trajectory 1960-64: fewer objects
arranged more densely and closer to the viewer, fugitive outlines, cast
shadows acquiring physical presence "as though they were additional objects,"
forms dissolving into surroundings. His own line on the setup: "The objects
were set out as if on a miniature stage, and to my eyes they looked strangely
false."

## What makes the work good (quality notes)

1. **He composes the intervals, not the objects.** His own complaint was
   always about "the spaces" between things, never the things. The gap, the
   overlap, and the shared edge are the primary variables; the bottles are the
   excuse. The generative translation: parameterize intervals (negative
   widths, overlap depth, how two silhouettes merge into one continuous
   contour) first, objects second.

2. **One cast, infinite recomposition.** The same bottles recur across decades
   in new combinations. Restricting the vocabulary to a handful of shapes is
   what let the differences become the subject. This is the Feldman/Satie
   logic from the restraint run: depth comes from recombining a tiny fixed
   set, not from novelty. A generative piece set should reuse one shape
   library across dozens of variants; the quality lives in permutation.

3. **Serial variation is the argument.** The yellow-cloth paintings only mean
   something together. Do not ship one "Morandi-like" image; ship the series,
   where faint shifts of tone, scale, and arrangement become the visible
   content.

4. **Remove the specular, keep the volumetric.** Painting bottles matte and
   filling glass with pigment was a deliberate deletion of highlights,
   reflections, and brand noise (labels), leaving only form, tone, and
   relation. For generative work: strip specular shine, texture realism, and
   decorative detail first; let shape relations carry everything. The white
   objects in the late paintings nearly dissolve into the ground, which proves
   the system works even at near-zero contrast.

5. **One continuous contour.** Objects are ganged so they touch, hiding and
   cropping one another until a single edge belongs to two forms and the back
   shapes flatten into cut-outs. In the 1948 inspection the group reads as one
   connected mass, not five vessels. This is the source of the "monumentality
   of his small, intimate subjects" the New Criterion names.

6. **The stage is shallow and fixed.** Restricted distance to the viewer, a
   flat table plane, an ambiguous wall behind, objects crowding the picture
   edge. The Tate pack names the edge as a structural device. Lock the camera,
   lock the horizon, lock the palette; vary only arrangement and interval.
   Tension comes from crowding the frame, never from camera drama.

7. **Shadows are members of the cast.** In the late work the cast shadows gain
   physical presence equal to the bottles; the caareviews essay notes the 1964
   shadow that "struggles to mimic any particular shape or fix itself to any
   one point." A system that treats shadows as a rendering afterthought misses
   half his compositional vocabulary.

8. **Refusal of narrative is the point of view.** Conventional titles only,
   milliners' dummies explicitly not symbolic, no faces, no people, none of
   the genre's traditional vanitas language. The New Criterion's key line:
   what makes a Morandi a Morandi is "the uncanny tensions of his
   compositions, the subtle relationships of his shapes, tonalities, and
   colors," not the bottles. A generator that adds props for storytelling has
   left the building.

9. **The narrowing field.** The visual arc across the seven inspections: 1914
   is fractured and wide-ranging (luminance std 52); by 1948 the range has
   compressed to a whisper (std 19) with everything one temperature apart; the
   1962 work re-widens only to place two dark punctuation bottles against the
   whitening rows; 1964 dissolves to three pale things barely holding their
   edges. His life's work is a narrowing. Restraint as a trajectory, not a
   style. A derivative piece that starts at the late whitening without earning
   the compression is borrowing the ending.

The 1918 metaphysical inspection matters for the lineage: the mannequin-head
object centered on a shallow boxed stage, flanked by dark panels, already
contains the mature species (one enigmatic object, a miniature stage, flat
planes) with de Chirico's theater drained out. He kept the stage and discarded
the enigma.

## Re-render findings

Study A, the grouping grammar. A1 (five vessels touching, overlapping into
one continuous contour) is the species. A2 (the same cast evenly spaced with
clear gaps) reads as an inventory lineup: the composition was in the contact,
not the objects. A3 (four touching, one set apart by a wide interval) makes
the gap an event; the apart vessel reads as the exile while the group holds.
A4 (the species with one vessel recolored saturated red) kills the picture
instantly; the red becomes the whole subject and the tonal negotiation
vanishes. Finding: the composition is contact plus interval; any accent louder
than a tonal step breaks the contract.

Study B, tonal compression. B1 (full tonal range with glossy highlight
stripes) reads as a product illustration; each object sits in its own light.
B2 (the same arrangement compressed into the narrow warm-gray band) is the
species; the objects negotiate with each other instead of performing. B3 (B2
with one dark accent) makes the accent the whole event, the 1962 punctuation
species: the eye goes to it immediately and the other three become its ground.
B4 (everything one tone, edges only) is the dissolution direction, the 1964
end: fragile, on the edge of disappearance, and the disappearance is the
point.

## Avoid-list additions (Morandi-specific)

- Bottles as a theme instead of bottles as a compositional system; the New
  Criterion explicitly mocks the "lined up in a row" reading.
- One image instead of a series; the method is permutational, a single
  arrangement says nothing.
- Decorative color or high contrast; his palette is earthy, greyed,
  fresco-dusted, and saturated accents are the anti-Morandi move.
- Shiny, reflective, photographic objects; he physically deleted exactly that.
- Narrative props and symbolism; no vanitas, no stories, no faces.
- Busy backgrounds and deep space; the room behind the bottles is the first
  decision, and it is always deletion.
- The "strangely false" stage without the discipline: staging objects
  theatrically without doing the interval work is set dressing, not Morandi.

## Honest gaps

No original handled. All seven images are 379-640px WikiArt web
reproductions; every color and tone judgment comes from JPEGs, and the tonal
statistics describe the reproductions, not the paintings. The "Still Life with
Five Objects, 1956" file is a cross-hatched black-and-white work read here as
his etching practice; the file page does not confirm the medium, so the
catalog attribution is uncertain. The biography is the English Wikipedia as
condensed in the delegated research dossier, and the dossier's own gaps apply:
ThoughtCo and Wikipedia disagree on the father's death year and the Academy
years; the "nothing is more abstract" line is attributed to three possibly
distinct interviews (1955, 1957, 1960) without resolution; the New Criterion
essay was paywalled past its opening; the long-looking anecdotes arrive
secondhand through Wilkin and Rewald. The early landscapes, the flower
paintings, and the watercolors were not visually inspected. The re-renders are
schematic species/control demonstrations, not reconstructions; pass 1 carried
the white-ellipse-outline artifact described above, fixed in pass 2.
