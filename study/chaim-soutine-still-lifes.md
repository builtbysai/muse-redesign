# Chaim Soutine: the still lifes (1893-1943)

Deep study, 2026-10-04. Hop-only session, queue exhausted. Named first among
next doorways by the 2026-10-04 Paul Cezanne study, carried through the
Chardin note before it. The quality-first choice after the long restraint
run: the master of the tilted plane and the single dead thing. Rembrandt is
his declared master (the Slaughtered Ox of 1655, watched in the Louvre until
it drove him mad), Chardin is his motif master (La Raie, re-staged), Cezanne
is his structural master (the tonal anchoring, with equilibrium rejected for
flux). The lineage of the still life passes through him into de Kooning.
What he offers generative work is not expressionism as a style but a
discipline of wrongness: one force field per composition, one figure per
canvas, and a mark-making system that behaves like flesh instead of
describing it.

## What the six inspections show

Carcass of Beef (MIA, 4847px): the single hanging vertical fills the frame
top to bottom, dead center. The meat is livid reds, pinks, and oranges with
the ribcage cavity opening at the middle; the top of the column is pale
yellow-white fat. The ground is a dark blue-green vortex, nearly black at
the edges, painted in agitated tangential strokes that churn around the
carcass rather than sitting behind it. The silhouette carries an impasto
halo: lighter dabs along the meat's edge that separate it from the dark
without a drawn contour. The subject is one thing, huge, alone; the ground
is the weather around it.

La Raie (Chartres, 2321px): the rayfish hangs vertically, the red tail
rising to the upper left, the pale pink triangular body filling the lower
two thirds, the red gill mass sitting like a wound at its center. The ground
is dark blue with the same churn as the carcass pictures. The Chardin
answer: Chardin's ray is pinned inside a crowded kitchen tableau; Soutine's
ray is isolated, floating, the only event on the canvas. Note the honest
gap: this is an install photo with a flash bloom in the upper area, so the
brightest passages of the file are glare, not paint.

Il Tacchino (2190px): the plucked turkey in yellows and oranges, the neck
and head hanging up into the top of the frame, the body a hot yellow mass
at center. A knife lies diagonally across the upper right, one of the few
props he ever allows, and it is allowed because it points: a straight pale
diagonal against the swirl. The dark blue-green ground churns again. The
photo's ornate gold frame is an honest-gap flag, not part of the picture.

L'artichaut (Zurich, 1004px): the sliding table in full. The tabletop is
tipped steeply, the rear edge climbing to the right and disappearing into
the dark; the front edge is a low arc across the bottom third. On it: a
brown jug leaning left, a small blue glass tipped slightly right, the
spiky green artichoke, a black bottle nearly upright, a plate of lemons
riding the table's own tilt, and in the foreground a fork lying flat,
pointing diagonally to the right. Every object has its own slide, but all
slides agree with one gravity: toward the viewer and the picture plane. The
objects touch and overlap; nothing sits in isolated inventory space.

Harengs et oignons (c.1917, 1500px): the early one, still Cézanne's
student. A tipped platter holds three silvery herrings laid as pale
diagonals across a deep red bed, with two onions. Brighter, more
constructed, less vortex: the palette has not yet gone dark. Useful as the
before picture, not as the species.

Violin, Bread, and Fish (c.1922, 640px): three long things as diagonals.
The violin is an electric orange-red mass lying from upper left to lower
right, f-holes visible; the baguette runs a near-parallel diagonal to its
left; a pale fish lies along the right edge. A chair spindle at left. The
ground is a brown-grey swirl. The composition is all diagonals in agreement,
the vortex before the carcass series tightened it into the vertical.

## The technique, as found

The dossier's condensed account: Sotheby's formulation, that bread and
crockery "are not arranged for quiet contemplation but animated by the
urgency of his brush. Colour does not describe; it animates. Forms tilt and
strain, as if unsettled from within"; Wheeler's recurrent "slant"; Waldemar
George's 1928 "wildness of rhythm loosed on the canvas," bending things "as
though they had St. Vitus' dance." The structural insight, Sotheby's again:
Soutine absorbed Cezanne's tonal anchoring of form "yet rejected equilibrium
in favour of volatility. Where Cezanne sought permanence through
construction, Soutine embraced flux." Frieze's technical read: "the
demonstrative warping of form ... exacerbated by the autonomous plasticity
of his thick impasto," strokes that "intermittently detach themselves from
their object, taking on a life of their own," "lacy trails of blue
scumbling" that "become a subject unto themselves." Yau's formula:
"destruction and renewal are different sides of the same coin." Ben-Meir's
two lines: he did not copy the old masters, he "would stage their subjects
for himself within his studio"; and forks are "subtly anthropomorphized,"
becoming "grasping hands, emaciated arms." The palette is a chord per
canvas: the famous reds, the putrid greens, the corpse blues, played in
different keys (Wheeler's two rayfish: pink and satiny vs. sharp red,
turquoise, yellow).

## What makes the work good (quality notes)

1. **One force field per composition.** The tilt is directional, not
   decorative. In the table still lifes everything slides along one visual
   gravity: toward the viewer, toward the plane, the rear edge climbing, the
   front edge sagging. The jug leans left, the glass tips right, the fork
   points across, but every displacement agrees with the field. In Study B
   the random-tilt control (B3) reads as clumsy and the correct-perspective
   control (B2) reads as dead. The generative rule: apply one displacement
   field to everything in the picture; never wobble each object its own way.

2. **One thing, huge, alone.** The rayfish floats isolated; the ox fills
   the frame as a single vertical; the turkey is the whole picture. He
   stages the object, then strips the stage: no butcher-shop props, no
   narrative context, no drapery. In Study A the horizontal control (A2)
   reads as a butcher's inventory photo and the two-carcass control (A4)
   reads as a shop window. The generative rule: build the picture around one
   figure and remove everything that is not it; the ground is weather, not
   scenery.

3. **The mark must BE the flesh, not depict it.** Frieze's observation is
   the center of his quality: strokes detach from their objects and take on
   a life of their own. A painterly filter on a fish photo is exactly the
   failure mode; the mark-making system must have its own behavior that the
   subject is handed over to. In Study A the clean-outline control (A3)
   reads as an illustration logo the moment the line precedes the marks.
   The generative rule: the marks are the medium the thing exists in, never
   a style applied afterward; let strokes disobey the subject's boundaries.

4. **The vertical is a figure; the horizontal is inventory.** The carcass
   series works because the meat stands like a crucifixion or a portrait.
   Laid down, the same meat is groceries. Orientation is meaning here, not
   layout. The generative rule: test the axis first; if the subject reads
   better lying down, it was never his subject.

5. **One tight palette, driven to saturation.** The reds, the corpse blues,
   the putrid greens: each canvas is a chord and the whole canvas is in the
   chord, fish and tablecloth alike. Sampling "Soutine colors" at random
   from many paintings gets mud; the lesson is one palette per piece, pushed
   until it saturates. The tonal measurements back it: the mature pictures
   compress to dark grounds (means in the 60s) so the hot chord can carry.

6. **Study masters by re-staging, not by copying.** Ben-Meir's line is the
   working method of the whole study program: Soutine did not copy
   Rembrandt's Ox or Chardin's Raie; he hung his own carcass and his own
   rayfish in his own studio and answered their gravity with his own. A
   derivative that copies his surfaces is doing the one thing he explicitly
   did not do to his masters. The generative rule: reconstruct the setup
   (one vertical figure, one force field, one chord), then make your own
   decisions inside it.

7. **Tender, not brutal.** Ben-Meir: the carcasses carry "a profound
   awareness of the inexorable processes of death, putrefaction and
   decay," yet the fish "thrum with formerly pulsing life"; Presutti's
   phrase, "the divine in decay." Gore as shock content, horror shorthand,
   or the tortured-artist anecdote as branding are the documented failure
   modes: the blood-and-formaldehyde story is partly legend, and the
   biography is the studio's electricity bill, not the light. Judge the
   tilt, the chord, the single figure. A derivative that leads with the
   butcher's-pail anecdote has missed the reverence.

8. **The ground must churn.** The dark field is not a backdrop; it is
   painted in agitated tangential strokes that swirl around the figure. A
   flat dark gradient behind a bright subject is a spotlight, not his
   picture. In Study A the species needed the vortex ground to hold; the
   figure alone on a flat ground reads as a specimen. The generative rule:
   the background gets its own stroke system, and it moves.

## Re-render findings

Study A, the carcass as the one vertical figure. A1 (one vertical column of
modulated reds and pinks on the churning dark ground, impasto halo along the
silhouette) is the species: it reads as a figure standing in weather. A2
(the same meat laid horizontal on a table band) reads as a butcher's
inventory photo: the subject is dead in the other sense. A3 (clean outlines,
smooth gradient shading, black contours) reads as an illustration logo: the
moment the line comes first, the facture dies. A4 (two carcasses side by
side) reads as a competition, a shop window: singularity is load-bearing.
Finding: verticality plus singularity plus the churning ground is the
composition; the vertical axis is the meaning, and the ground must move.

Study B, the sliding table. B1 (tipped table, rear edge climbing, each
object at its own slight slide, objects touching and overlapping) is the
species: the tension reads. B2 (correct one-point perspective, plumb
objects, level fork) reads as a competent illustration: nothing is wrong
and nothing is alive. B3 (random per-object tilts, the fork sticking up
oddly) reads as clumsy, not meaningful: the wrongness must be exact per
object and must agree with the field. B4 (objects spaced evenly, no contact)
reads as a product lineup. Finding: the composition is one force field
applied to everything, with objects locked to their own slides inside it;
random wobble is the documented failure mode of the imitator, and
inventory spacing is the failure mode of the decorator. Caveat: the
table-plane dabs were not clipped to the polygon, so the plane bleeds into
the ground; the sheet is a schematic demonstration, single pass.

## Avoid-list additions (Soutine-specific)

- Arbitrary distortion presented as his wrongness; every displacement must
  agree with one force field per composition.
- Gore, horror, or shock as the subject; the carcasses are tender and
  melancholic, the divine in decay, not brutality.
- Painterly filters or distortion shaders on a photographed subject passed
  off as impasto; the marks must have their own behavior, applied before
  and beneath the subject, never after it.
- Clean outlines drawn first and filled after; the line must never precede
  the marks.
- Two or more competing figures in one frame; singularity is load-bearing.
- Flat dark backgrounds behind a bright subject; the ground must churn with
  its own stroke system.
- Random color sampling from his paintings as a palette; one tight chord
  per piece, driven to saturation.
- The tortured-artist anecdote as branding for the piece; the blood story
  is partly legend and the biography is not the light.
- Copying his surfaces (the reds, the carcass) instead of re-staging his
  setup (one figure, one field, one chord).
