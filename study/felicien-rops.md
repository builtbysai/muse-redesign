# Felicien Rops, the flat color ground and the sower in the sky (Les Sataniques, 1882)

Deep study, 2026-10-06. Hop-only session, queue exhausted. Named as
the next doorway by the 2026-10-06 Kubin study (Rops was named in
Kubin's own 1898-99 Munich print-room encounter, and the dossier
documents the Rops-Kubin line; the Rops-Redon pairing is critical
construction, not a documented exchange). The quality-first choice:
Rops is the Belgian symbolist intaglio master at the exact node where
the lineage's print studies kept pointing, and his color Les
Sataniques turn out to be a compositional species the lineage had not
seen: the committed ground in COLOR, with the wash forbidden to model
anything.

## Sources

Biography, technique, publishing facts, works, the Kubin and Redon
links, criticism, conflicts, and gaps via a delegated research
dossier (text-only pass; all nine visual inspections and both
re-render families are mine). Key facts, carried:

- Biography: born 7 July 1833, Namur, Belgium, textile-manufacturing
  family. Founded the satirical weekly L'Uylenspiegel 1856 with
  Charles De Coster (180+ lithographs). Met Baudelaire's publisher
  Poulet-Malassis 1862; Baudelaire wrote to Manet in 1865 that
  "Rops is the only true artist... that I have found in Belgium."
  Permanent move to Paris around 1874; by 1877 boasting he was the
  best-paid illustrator in France. Died 23 August 1898 at Essonnes.
- Technique: lithography first (to about 1865), then etching studied
  in Paris 1862 with Bracquemond and Jacquemart, the French revival's
  central figures. Britannica's verdict: "a printmaker of brilliant
  technique" whose drypoint marks him "one of the masters of the
  medium," and one of the first modern etchers to revive soft-ground
  etching. Mediums: etching, drypoint, soft-ground, aquatint,
  mezzotint, burin, heliogravure. The characteristic move was
  combination: heliogravure transfer of his own drawings, then
  hand rework in drypoint, aquatint, or soft-ground. With Armand
  Rassenfosse he invented "Ropsenfosse," several soft-ground
  formulas, likely the first soft-ground method for color prints
  from two or more plates.
- Publishing: 34 frontispieces for books between 1864 and 1870 alone.
  Founded the Societe internationale des aquafortistes in Brussels
  around 1869 to bring French techniques to Belgium; it failed
  financially after two or three years. For Les Diaboliques he
  delegated the small-board etching, disliked the result ("the small
  boards do not say anything"), and reworked large heliogravure
  boards by hand with aquatint, soft-ground, and drypoint for the
  nocturnal effects he wanted. 1911 Britannica: "Hardly any artist of
  the 19th century equalled him in the use of the dry-point and soft
  varnish."
- Les Sataniques (1882): his own initiative, independent of book
  commissions, conceived 1878-79, originally meant to be twelve
  plates with texts on a modern woman's infatuation with the devil,
  possibly part of a larger Album du Diable never completed. Five
  heliogravures published 1882-1895, from original drawings: Satan
  semant l'ivraie, L'enlevement, Le Calvaire, L'idole, Le Sacrifice.
  Each about 28 x 20 cm, often signed in red crayon. The 2007
  Sotheby's sale of the original watercolor for Le Calvaire fetched
  about 162,500 pounds.
- The Kubin link, documented: a LeClaire Kunst catalogue text states
  that in Munich around 1898-99 Kubin encountered the work of
  "Max Klinger. He also encountered the work of Goya, Redon, Ensor
  and Rops" in the Alte Pinakothek graphic arts collection; a
  caareviews review of the Hoberg research confirms "his exposure
  to and subsequent preoccupation with the graphic works of Max
  Klinger, Francisco de Goya, and Felicien Rops." Wikipedia lists
  Kubin among the younger artists Rops's prints influenced (Beckmann,
  Corinth, Ensor, Khnopff, Klinger, Munch among the rest).
- The Redon link, honestly marked: NO documented direct influence
  line in either direction, no letter, no stated encounter. Both
  attended Mallarme's Tuesdays; Redon later joined Les XX. Both
  illustrated Flaubert's Tentation de Saint Antoine (Rops 1878,
  Redon's series 1888). The pairing is a curator's construction:
  the French symbolist counterpoint to the Belgian. Redon worked
  charcoal and lithography; Rops worked intaglio and heliogravure.
  Opposite technical temperaments inside the same symbolist print
  culture.
- Criticism: Huysmans credited him with reclaiming lust for the
  visual arts, "not obscene and practical but Catholic, flaming and
  terrible." Fenelon: "who paints phalluses the way others paint
  landscapes." Mirbeau: "Painter, man of letters, philosopher,
  scholar, Rops was all of that." La Plume devoted a special tribute
  number to him, No. 172, 15 June 1896. The dossier's honest verdict:
  art historians do not consider him a seminal figure even among
  the symbolists, yet "no creditable review of symbolism could
  ignore him."

## Visual inspections (9)

1. Le Sacrifice, aquarelle (Commons 1680x2618, from the Neyer 1999
   Wilhelm-Busch-Museum catalogue): the skull-headed horned figure
   with the thin red halo, the nude woman on the altar block, two
   small winged skull-figures at the sides, the carved crouching
   figure on the altar front. The ground is one flat teal-gray wash
   to every edge; the figures are drawn in line and reserved pale;
   the darks are rationed (dark fraction 0.017).
2. Satan semant l'ivraie, aquarelle (Commons 1798x2600): the giant
   striding through the sky in a wide-brimmed hat, one arm flung
   out sowing small figures, the pale moon at upper right, the city
   below as a dark band with no windows and no roofs, the small
   figures scattered along the sowing path. The giant never touches
   the ground. Dark fraction 0.015.
3. Le Calvaire, aquarelle (Commons 1816x2618): the crucified
   goat-demon with the "BELZ" titulus, the nude woman below with
   the black drapery, the candle row. The ground is full-bleed RED,
   one flat value to every edge, the darkest of the four (mean
   116.3, std 24.4, the most compressed). Signed "F. Rops" bottom
   left.
4. L'enlevement, aquarelle (Commons 1734x2609): two nude figures
   flying on broomsticks against a flat blue sky, one long black
   streamer, one pale tail. Almost no dark at all (dark fraction
   0.000, mean 159.3, std 25.0). The sky is never modeled.
5. Le Sacrifice, etching (Met 49.55.286 DP234476, ca. 1882): the
   same composition as the aquarelle, confirmed plate for plate
   (the skull head, the halo, the altar, the side figures), which
   verifies the colored versions as Rops's own hand. The plate is
   a small vignette centered on a large sheet, signed in red
   pencil below the platemark. Mean 128.8 on the plate area.
6. Satan creant les monstres, soft-ground etching (LACMA via
   Commons 1920x1278): the dark winged demon crouching, wings
   spread as a horizontal dark band touching both edges, the
   figure the one dark event on an even mottled gray ground
   (mean 117.4, dark fraction 0.055).
7. Canicule, etching (Met 49.55.284 DP234474, ca. 1882): the
   sleeping nude in a few drypoint strokes with a diagonal hatch
   above, the plate a small vignette on the big sheet, signed in
   red pencil, the title written in the margin bottom right. The
   palest and most compressed plate measured (mean 216.0, std
   15.4).
8. Modernite, drypoint (Met 2012.136.493, 1883): the elegant woman
   in the hat holding a platter with a severed academician's head,
   the "ACADEMIE" ribbon, loose sketchy drypoint on a toned sheet,
   the plate nearly full-bleed, signed "83" in the plate and "F.R"
   in pencil below.
9. The Lace-Maker, charcoal study (Met 2016.693, 1876): the seated
   woman in costume with the lace pillow, window and plant behind,
   working notes in the margins ("16 x 60"). The counter-species:
   full tonal modeling in charcoal, the one sheet where he works
   the other way (mean 145.6, std 59.5, the widest range measured).

## Re-render families (PIL, single pass, crude glyphs)

Family A, the flat color ground. A1: flat single-color wash
full-bleed, the line-drawn pale figure, one dark accent, is the
species. A2: the same with a vertical gradient ground is the
named filter failure, an Instagram preset, not a Rops. A3: the
figure modeled in the wash (a chiaroscuro blob around it) is the
spotlight failure. A4: texture everywhere drowns the figure.
Findings: the wash must be one flat value to every edge; the
drawing must be line only; the wash is never allowed to model
form.

Family B, the sower in the sky. B1: the giant striding in the
sky, the city as a dark band with no windows, the small figures
scattered along the sowing path, the one pale moon, is the
species. B2: the giant standing with its feet on the city is the
domestication failure, the sowing becomes a pose. B3: the city
rendered with windows and roofs is the inventory failure. B4: the
small figures in an even grid is the specimen failure. Findings:
the giant must be airborne (no ground contact, no floor, no
shadow); the city must be a band, not a place; the small figures
get a trajectory, not a mass or a grid; the moon is the one pale
punctuation.

## Quality thesis

Rops's Les Sataniques exist in two registers, and the color
register is the original: the aquarelles are the source drawings,
the heliogravures the reproductions. Both registers obey the same
discipline, but the color one states it more radically. The ground
commits to ONE flat color value across the whole sheet, red or
blue or teal, and the wash is forbidden to model anything. All
form is line. All light is reserve. The darks are rationed to one
or two accents per sheet. This is the committed-ground finding
from the Kubin study, but in color, and with a harder rule: in
Kubin the wash could be weather; in Rops the wash is not even
weather, it is a decision. The sower plate adds the second
thesis: the giant in the sky, never grounded, sowing the small
figures along a path, the city reduced to a band, one moon. It
is the scale inversion of the Kubin study set in motion: the
giant does not loom over the small, it passes through their sky.
The etching presentation states the third thesis: the plate as a
small vignette on the large sheet, signed in red pencil below the
platemark, the margin load-bearing and the signature the one warm
punctuation. What makes the work good is restraint at every
level: the ground decides once, the line draws once, the dark
appears once.

## Avoid-list additions

- Gradient or modeled color grounds as his color practice; the
  wash never models form, never grades, never textures.
- The satanic and erotic signifiers as the subject (skulls,
  demons, nudes, the BELZ titulus) without the ground and line
  discipline; the motifs are decoration on the structure.
- Rendering the city under the giant; grounding the giant; giving
  it a floor or a shadow.
- The small figures massed or gridded; they are sown, they get a
  trajectory.
- The red pencil signature treated as a quirk; it is the
  punctuation outside the plate.
- The Rops-Redon influence line presented as documented; it is
  critical construction.
- "L'idole" vs "Satan creant les monstres" as settled titles; the
  Les Sataniques date (1880/1882/1883); the twelve-plate plan as
  fact; the 1990 Galerie St. Etienne show date already corrected
  in the Kubin study.
- The 1911 Britannica's "dry-point" wording taken as a technique
  claim; period sources use the term loosely.

## Honest gaps

No original handled; the four aquarelles are book reproductions
from the 1999 Neyer catalogue at 1680-1816 px, color balance
unverified against the sheets. The LACMA monstres file is a
1920px render of the tif. Only two of the five Sataniques plates
seen as etchings (Sacrifice, via the Met); L'idole not located,
and whether "Satan creant les monstres" is a variant title of
L'idole is unverified. The dossier's gaps are carried (the
Ropsenfosse working method, the Diaboliques edition dispute, the
Exsteens 784-787 numbering oddity, the Kubin encounter resting on
a dealer catalogue and a review). Re-renders are single-pass
schematics with crude glyphs.
