# Hanne Darboven: Study Notes

**Profile:** Hanne Darboven (Munich, 1941, to Hamburg, March 9, 2009).
Painter turned conceptual artist in New York 1966-1968, where she met
Sol LeWitt, Carl Andre, and Joseph Kosuth and began the Konstruktionen.
Four decades at a desk in Harburg, near Hamburg, writing time by hand:
pen, pencil, typewriter, graph paper, vellum, index cards. Her material
is the Gregorian calendar reduced to one private arithmetic, and her
subject is the passing of time measured at human scale. (The Galerie
Greta Meert CV prints "b. 1947" which is a typo; the Menil Drawing
Institute guide and standard biographies give 1941.)

**Depth:** deep on text, deep on three images, three local re-render
studies. Read in full: the Menil Drawing Institute "Writing Time"
gallery guide (2023-24 exhibition, English and Spanish texts, complete);
Cathleen Chaffee, "i can't describe so i will write: Hanne Darboven's
Abstract Correspondence" (Castelli Gallery, A Survey, 2013, read to the
end of the essay body, footnotes not chased); Wikipedia's Mathematical
Music, Wunschkonzert, and Kulturgeschichte passages; the Artmap Deutsche
Guggenheim 2006 text on the music system; the Reina Sofia EVOL
performance page for Opus 17a. Three images visually inspected at full
resolution: Construction/Perforation New York 1966/67 (Spruch Magers
file, 1664x1890), the Ein Jahrhundert installation view at Spruth
Magers (1890x1260), Ohne Titel Model I Serie 4 1966/67 (metropolkunstraum
file, 535x528). NOT seen: Six Books on 1968 in full, the Menil
Construction/Perforation detail photograph, any Wunschkonzert page at
reading resolution, any performance of the Mathematical Music, the
Homage to the Odyssey vellum panels in person, any Hamburg-era video.

## The K-value engine: a checksum for each day

Her whole practice runs on one calculation. Take a date, add its digits,
call the sum the K-value, for Konstruktionen. January 1, 1968: 1+1+6+8 =
16, written 16K. The full year 1969 runs 1/1/69 to 31/12/69, which is
17K to 58K (Castelli's figures check out under her convention, K = day + month +
year-digits: 1+1+6+9 = 17, 31+12+6+9 = 58. The same convention gives
1/1/68 = 16, the century's last day 31/12/99 = 61, and the postcard
sums 29+1+7+4 = 41 and 7+9+7+5 = 28.)

The K then becomes marks. Often she wrote a day's K as that many rows
of her signature wave script: uuuuuuu. The u is an inverted 10, a tally
mark pretending to be a letter. Chaffee's reading is the useful one:
the repeated u is recognizable as writing and illegible as language, so
it invokes the whole alphabet and the condition of reading at once. In
Untitled ca. 1971-73 she drew a century this way, sets of wavelike lines
separated by semicolons, each curved section counting one K, the last
day of the century reaching 61K (12+31+9+9 = 61, this one checks out).

The re-render confirmed the part no text states plainly. Digit-sum
checksums are not monotonic, so the month's wave-bands swell and then
collapse: in my March 1969 sheet the bands deepen from 19K to 27K
across the month, then drop back to 19K on the 10th because the day
number gains a digit. The sawtooth is the composition. Time accumulates,
then the calendar resets it. That is the whole "visualization of the
flow of time" in one mechanism.

## Konstruktionen 1966-67: the number as a line

Before the dates came the Constructions. Fixed number patterns mapped
onto graph paper as diagonal lines that increase and decrease in length
in sequence. In Construction/Perforation New York 1966/67 (pencil and
ballpoint on graph paper, 29.5 x 27 in.) the Menil describes the
sequence as five graph squares long, then ten, then nine, then back to
five. Small pinholes in the sheet mark where each counting cycle begins
and ends. Variations: some write the sequences out as text, some drop
the line entirely and show only the paper with hole-punched patterns.

My first re-render misread this badly. I drew stiff vertical A-shaped
slabs and it looked like clip art. The second pass, done against the
reference pixels, gets the species: horizontal bands of parallel
diagonal ticks, each tick's length cycling 5, 10, 9, 5 graph squares,
slope alternating every four ticks so the band folds into shallow
chevrons, pinholes at the cycle boundaries. The finding that matters:
the line length is the composition. Nothing else moves. The ticks are
an amplitude envelope drawn one stroke at a time, and the chevron fold
keeps a single slope from going decorative.

## Write without describing

Her statements circle one refusal. "I do like to write and I don't
like to read / one plus one is one two." To Artforum in 1973: the
mathematical system is "a way of writing without describing." To
LeWitt: "just take / everyday's mathematical / index, a great
invention, fiction. / No inquiry, no research, no / exploration, just
to search into / something between everything / for a time while /
time is going on. / Don't write words, numbers / in a constructive
way any / more, just measure: lines." To Gisbourne in the 1990s:
"There is never any sense of writing for others, everything is written
for myself alone... never apologize and never explain," a line she
took from Carl Andre and repeated daily.

The correspondence practice is where this stops being theory. She sent
cards to Castelli, LeWitt, and dealers that are laid out like letters
but contain almost no legible content. The January 29, 1974 card to
Castelli: the date written out, "29+1+7+4=41," then u-waves and
redundant German and English prose ("cassius clay is / gewinnt winning
/ Bessie Smith is singt singing / uuuuuuu am / schreibe writing").
The September 7, 1975 index card: "dear leo, uuuuuuu ... 7+9+7+5=28 /
today [heute], love hanne." The September 25, 1984 graph-paper sheet:
rows of u's headed "sunrise/sunset to leo Sept 25, 1984 / with love
from hanne today." Chaffee notes we search the u's for meaning because
the letter format demands it, and in the sunrise/sunset sheet the
framing text makes our eyes conjure a horizon line inside the graphic
field, "as if we are squinting at the sun." The invitation to read and
the refusal to describe, on the same sheet.

## Mathematical Music: the date as a chord

Late 1970s, with her pianist's training, she built a notation from the
same number systems. Her rule, from the sources: digit 1 is the note e,
2 is f, 3 is g, and so on up the scale. Compound numbers become
intervals of two notes, 31 is g-e, 24 is f-h. Numbers containing 0 are
broken chords. "My systems are numeric concepts that work according to
the laws of progression and/or reduction in the manner of a musical
theme with variations." With a collaborator she adapted the scores
into performable pieces for organ, double bass, harpsichord, string
quartet, chamber orchestra.

Wende 80 (1980), built on an interview with Helmut Schmidt and Franz
Josef Strauss, is the first work that is simultaneously a musical
score; the music was pressed on 11 LPs in a black case, edition of 250.
Wunschkonzert (1984) is the monument: 1008 pages in uniform size,
divided into 4 opus groups (Opus 17a and b, 18a and b), each opus 36
poems of 6 pages plus a title page carrying an antiquarian
confirmation greeting card. Opus 17a is a cello score of about 100
minutes; Robert Black recorded it for the DIA Art Foundation in 2007.
Since 2014 the duo EVOL (Roc Jimenez de Cisneros and Stephen Sharp)
have built computer-music variations on the score and performed them
at Unsound and the Reina Sofia.

My staff re-render of 29/1/1974 under her rule gives f+f', e, e+f',
d'+a. Dates become clusters, mostly dissonant, with the year always
the busiest chord. The mapping is deterministic and the harmony is an
accident of the calendar, which is exactly her aesthetic: the system
decides, the ear takes what it gets.

## The crossed-out today

Nearly every sheet of the late installations ends by writing "today"
in English and German (heute), then crossing it out with a single
line. In Inventions That Have Changed Our World (1996), ten inventor
figurines from Gutenberg to the Wright brothers represent the 20th
century while the word today is written and struck, sheet after sheet.
The Menil's reading: the present is always there and not there,
receding into the past as soon as it is written. One firm line. No
drama in the gesture, all of it in the repetition.

## What makes it sing

The private logic is the work. The K-value, the tick lengths, the
digit-to-note map: each is a small arbitrary rule applied without
exception across an enormous surface. The surfaces stay modest
(graph paper, vellum, index cards, A4 sheets), the accumulation does
the monument work. The hand is always visible: rows of u's that no two
alike, ticks that wobble off the grid. And the system has cracks she
never patched. The Menil is blunt that her math sometimes fails, and
she kept the failures in. The u pretends to be a letter and refuses to
be one. The struck-out today is the only emotional gesture in the
whole practice, and she repeats it thousands of times.

## Avoid-list additions

- u-wave script without a counting rule behind it. The waves are a
  checksum, not decoration. Rows of u's with no K are wallpaper.
- Graph paper as set dressing. Hers is the instrument's surface, with
  the digits written in the margins and the pinholes marking cycles.
- Checksum aesthetics without the private logic's occasional breaks.
  Her math sometimes fails and she kept the failures in the work.
- Illegibility as mystique. Hers is specific: one letter that invokes
  the whole alphabet while refusing to be read.
- Crossing out as angst. Hers is time passing: one firm line, repeated
  daily, no drama in the gesture.
- Dates-as-notes without her progression and reduction law. The music
  is theme and variations, not a cipher toy.

## Honest gaps

- The Castelli 17K-58K range for 1969 could not be reproduced from
  digit sums (digit sums give 17K-22K); the summing convention is
  unrecovered. Menil's note that her math often fails is the only
  cover for this.
- Konstruktionen: the exact original number sequences are known only
  through the Menil's 5/10/9/5 example; no program or key survives in
  the sources read.
- No Wunschkonzert page seen at reading resolution; the 0-as-broken-
  chord rule is stated by sources but no scored example was inspected.
- No performance of the Mathematical Music heard; EVOL's
  reinterpretations known only from the Reina Sofia event page.
- Homers Odyssee known only through the Castelli caption (fourteen
  panels, ~500 vellum sheets); the vellum transcription never seen.
- Study A (march1969 page) fit only 16 of 31 day-bands before
  overflowing; the heute footer collided with the bands, an artifact
  of my layout, not her practice. Study C's title line carries my own
  typo ("darwoben") in the local evidence file.
