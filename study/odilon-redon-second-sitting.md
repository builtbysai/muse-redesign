# Odilon Redon, second sitting: the black sun, the seam, and the color turn (1878-1914)

Deep study, 2026-10-06. Hop-only session, queue exhausted. Named as
the first next doorway by the 2026-10-06 Rodolphe Bresdin study. A
second sitting after the 2026-10-04 noir study (study/odilon-redon.md),
which established the field thesis: one form suspended in one composed
field, the value key free, the light as the subject, legibility
rationed. That thesis stands. This pass asks what it does not cover:
the inverted light source, the diagonal value seam, the scattered-eye
mode, and the color turn the first study set aside by design. Ten new
works visually inspected at source resolution; Caliban and the crying
spider re-inspected as controls against the first pass.

Key facts, new or corrected against the 2026-10-04 note:

- Fantin-Latour, resolved: the first study flagged the
  Fantin-Latour lithography-suggestion claim as dubious against the
  Bresdin account. The new dossier confirms both are true. Bresdin
  taught Redon etching and lithography in Bordeaux (the meeting
  year is 1862 per the MoMA 1952 bulletin vs 1863 elsewhere,
  unresolved); Fantin-Latour suggested lithography in 1878 as the
  way to multiply his charcoal drawings. The procedural difference
  is documented: Bresdin's lithography was pen-and-ink dense;
  Redon's used the lithographic crayon for broad tonal masses that
  echo charcoal.
- The slave-trade story, struck: the first study carried as
  Wikipedia fact the claim that Redon's father made his fortune in
  the Louisiana slave trade and Redon was conceived in New Orleans.
  The new dossier marks it legendary. No scholarly source
  consulted (Wikipedia, the Art Institute catalogue, MoMA, the
  Dayton Art Institute) repeats it; it comes from one biography
  site. It is struck from the carried facts.
- Birth date still unresolved: 20 April (Wikipedia, WikiArt) vs
  22 April 1840 (Simple English Wikipedia); neither cites the
  Bordeaux civil record.
- Bresdin, the return line: Redon organized Bresdin's first
  retrospective at the 1908 Salon d'Automne (verified). The
  contents of Redon's catalogue text were not found in this pass;
  do not quote what has not been seen.
- Technique: the Getty's Apparition uses six distinct black
  drawing materials plus a yellow pastel (brown-black vine charcoal
  and black pastel layered for depth); intermittent fixative front
  and back per the dossier. The noirs are multi-material
  constructions, not monochrome improvisations. Redon's own line,
  re-carried: "Black is the most essential colour... Nothing
  prostitutes it... the agent of the spirit."
- Album plate counts, verified against the 1929 Art Institute
  catalogue and the MoMA 1952 bulletin: Dans le reve 10 plus
  frontispiece (1879, Lemercier, edition 25); A Edgar Poe 6 plus
  frontispiece (1882); Les Origines 8 plus frontispiece (1883);
  Hommage a Goya 6 (1885); La Nuit 6 (1886); Tentation de
  saint Antoine 10 plus frontispiece (1888), 6 plus frontispiece
  (1889), 24 (1896); Songes 6 (1891). Middle-album edition sizes
  are unverified beyond Dans le reve's 25.
- Rops verdict: confirmed construction. No documented contact,
  correspondence, or stated admiration between Rops and Redon was
  found; only shared circles (both in Les XX, both in the Huysmans
  A rebours orbit). The influence line stays marked as a critical
  construction, not a documented fact.
- Breton verdict: precursor status is institutional consensus
  (MoMA, Wikipedia), but no direct Breton-on-Redon quotation was
  verified in this pass. The personal-cult image is popular writing
  until a primary citation is produced.

## The artworks (all visually inspected at source resolution)

1. Le Noye (The Drowned Man in water by moonlight), charcoal,
   Rijksmuseum RP-T-1952-398, 4352x5856. The moon is a BLACK disc
   cropped by the top edge, with radiating stroke bursts; the field
   is a worked mid-dark gray; the drowned head is a tiny pale
   reserve at the bottom right, barely there. The inversion is the
   composition: the brightest object in the sky is the darkest mark,
   and the picture's light is the pale field around it.
2. Cellule Auriculaire, lithograph, Google Art Project, 2242x2401.
   A profile head reserved pale inside a near-black circular cell
   on a mid-gray lithographic grain ground; two pale accents (a
   finger-like form upper left, a small light at right) sit outside
   the cell. The cell is a black sun with a face in it.
3. Tentation de Saint-Antoine, first album, Pl. IV ("Il hausse le
   vase d'airain"), lithograph 1888, BnF scan, 2680x3924. A
   diagonal seam: pale worked left, a black wedge entering from the
   top right and owning the right third to the edges. The pale
   woman stands left, the bearded vessel-raiser at the seam. The
   caption sits below the plate. The seam does the lighting design.
4. Mephistopheles, charcoal, Google Art Project, 4651x5291. On
   brown paper: the face reserved pale in a worked brown-black
   hatched ground, one clawed finger touching the chin, the eyes
   large and sidelong. The ground is hatched all over; the figure
   is what the hatching spares.
5. L'araignee qui pleure (The Crying Spider), 1881, charcoal,
   3559x4666 (re-inspected as a control; matches the 2026-10-04
   lithograph reading). One dense dark furry mass, eight thin
   single-stroke legs reaching the frame edges, a faint tiled floor
   at the bottom, the field pale and barely worked. Confirms the
   light-ground species.
6. Le Cyclope, c. 1914, oil, 2846x3579. The one-eyed gold head in
   the sky upper left, the body merging into dark violet hill
   masses, the tiny pale sleeper bottom right. The eye is the
   single loaded event; the scale contrast (giant head, tiny
   sleeper) does the sublime. Broken pastel handling carried into
   oil: violet darks, pale blue-gray sky.
7. Les Yeux clos, 1890, oil, Google Art Project, 3822x4723. The
   closed-eye head centered, cropped at the bottom by a luminous
   horizontal band; blue-gray field, the body dissolving into
   light. The refusal of the gaze as composition.
8. Le Bouddha, pastel, Google Art Project, 3472x4430. The monk
   small at the left third, the massive dark tree trunk as a
   right-hand diagonal, blue-violet misty middle, yellow flower
   clusters upper left, small circular cellule motifs in the sky.
   Complementary discipline: blue-violet against gold, the dark
   mass rationed to one trunk.
9. Pegasus and Bellerophon, charcoal, Met DP359021, 2782x3877. The
   wing as ONE black mass, the horse head and the nude Bellerophon
   as pale reserves on the brown paper ground, the ground barely
   worked. The dark is rationed to the wing.
10. Head framed by black hair, wide-open eyes, lithograph,
    Rijksmuseum RP-T-1954-177, 3676x5450. Loose open crayon: the
    face mostly paper, the hair a dark scribbled mass, the eyes
    staring out. The late loose species: reserve does the work, the
    mark only the hair.
11. "Et des yeux sans tete flottaient comme des mollusques," A
    Edgar Poe plate, 862x1200 (small-file caveat). Disembodied eyes
    at receding scales on near-paper, one eel-like form with a
    face, minimal dark accents. The eye as a scattered population,
    not a single event.
12. Caliban, charcoal, Google Art Project, 1989x2757 (re-inspected
    as a control; matches the 2026-10-04 reading: full-bleed
    velvety black, the pale body lifted out, edges lost).

## Re-render studies (PIL schematics, single pass, visually inspected)

Family A, the committed ground. A1 (species): the full-bleed
worked dark grain plus the pale reserved face. A2: the vignette
spotlight around an outlined figure, the named filter failure. A3:
the even mid fill with a dark silhouette, the overcast failure.
A4: the contour drawing with web and bat signifiers, the
illustration failure. Findings: the ground must commit to a value
at the edges; the figure must be reserve, not outline; signifiers
without value discipline read as Halloween.

Family B, the apparition. B1 (species): the black disc in a pale
worked field plus the dark water band with horizontal strokes plus
the tiny pale head (the Noye grammar). B2: the centered disc, the
emblem and stamp failure. B3: the eye grid, the wallpaper failure.
B4: the pale rayed moon with rendered clouds and hills, the
conventional-illustration failure. Findings: the disc must be black
(the inversion is the device); it must sit off-center in a worked
field; the small pale incident at the bottom is load-bearing; a
glowing moon lets you fake the lighting, a black moon forces you
to compose it.

## Quality notes (what is new against the 2026-10-04 study)

- The inversion device: Redon draws the light source black (Le
  Noye's moon, the Cellule's cell, and by extension the
  Eye-Balloon as the dark event on the mid field). This is a
  separate device from the reserve figure. The reserve says the
  light is what you spare; the inversion says the light is what you
  darken. A derivative that draws a glowing moon has kept the
  subject and lost the discipline.
- The seam grammar: the two-field split with the figures at the
  seam (Tentation Pl. IV, measured). The first study's species was
  one field, one form; the seam is two fields, and the composition
  is the meeting. The failure mode is blending the seam into a
  gradient.
- The color turn keeps the discipline: Cyclope, Bouddha, Yeux clos
  all obey the field thesis (one composed field, the value key
  free, the subject as reserve or density event, the dark rationed
  to one mass). There is no break, only the same instrument in
  pastel and oil. The new failure mode this names: treating the
  color work as a different artist.
- The eye has two modes: the single loaded event (Cyclope, the
  Eye-Balloon) and the scattered population (the Poe plate). The
  scattered mode fails the moment the eyes align into a pattern.
- The loose late line (the black-haired head): the mark reduced to
  the hair mass, the face as pure reserve. Restraint at the drawing
  level, matching Arbre's restraint at the composition level.

What would make a derivative fail: a glowing or pale moon; the
black disc centered as an emblem; eyes repeated as pattern; the
seam blended into a gradient; the color work read as a different
artist; spooky signifiers without the value discipline; two or
more competing figures; detail spread evenly; a title that
explains the image.

## Avoid-list additions

- The light source drawn glowing or pale (the inversion is the
  device; a luminous moon is the conventional failure).
- The black disc centered as an emblem or stamp.
- Eyes repeated as pattern or wallpaper.
- The seam blended into a gradient.
- The color turn treated as a different artist or a decline
  narrative.
- The Louisiana slave-trade and New Orleans conception story
  (marked legendary by the new dossier; struck from the carried
  facts).
- The Rops-Redon influence line (confirmed construction; no
  documented contact, correspondence, or stated admiration).
- A personal Breton cult of Redon stated as fact (institutional
  precursor consensus only; no primary quote verified).
