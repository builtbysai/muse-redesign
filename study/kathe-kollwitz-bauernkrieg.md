# Kaethe Kollwitz, Bauernkrieg (Peasants' War) cycle, 1902-1908

Deep study, 2026-10-05. Hop-only session, queue exhausted. Named as
next doorway by the 2026-10-05 Otto Dix Der Krieg study ("Kollwitz's
Bauernkrieg as a serial study") and by the 2026-10-05 Max Beckmann
study, and first among the print-lineage studies' doorways that close
the war-print run (Goya's Desastres, Dix's Der Krieg, and now the
German print between them). The quality-first choice after the long
lineage run because it is the hinge the twentieth century actually
stood on: Dix built Der Krieg as a conscious sequence with a stated
Goya debt, but the sequence he was answering may have been
Kollwitz's, the one German war cycle every German etcher knew. The
dossier found no artist-stated link from either of them to the other,
which is itself the honest finding: the lineage is compositional,
not declared. The serial-study method keeps paying: this cycle's
value arc is as composed as the Caprichos and as deliberate as
Der Krieg, and it works the opposite way.

## Sources

Biography, technique, publishing facts, criticism, the lineage
question, conflicts, and gaps via a delegated research dossier
(text-only pass; the visual inspections are mine). Key facts, carried:

- Biography: born Kaethe Schmidt, 8 July 1867, Koenigsberg. Fifth
  child of a Social Democrat mason and a pastor's daughter, the
  socialist free-religious household the root of the political art.
  First lessons with engraver Rudolf Maurer 1881-82, School for Women
  Artists Berlin under Karl Stauffer-Bern 1885-86, Munich under
  Herterich 1888-90 where she concluded she was a draughtswoman not a
  painter. No formal printmaking instruction; self-taught from
  printers, manuals, and trial. The acknowledged early master is Max
  Klinger, whose etchings gave her both technique and social themes.
  Married Karl Kollwitz 1891; his working-class Prenzlauer Berg
  practice supplied her models and subjects. Taught drawing and
  etching 1898-1902/03, Berlin Secession member 1901-1913.
- The cycle's trigger, three strands: childhood barricade games with
  her brother Konrad and the legendary Black Anna (Schwarze Anna)
  figure she identified with; Gerhart Hauptmann's Florian Geyer
  (premiered 4 January 1896), hedged in every source as influence,
  treat as probable not documented; Wilhelm Zimmermann's history
  (1841-1843, Stuttgart) behind Hauptmann, no source showing Kollwitz
  read it herself.
- Why six years: an early proof of the Charge (Losbruch) at the sixth
  Berlin Secession show 1902 got positive reviews for the Black Anna
  figure; in 1904 the Association for Historic Art commissioned the
  remaining six plates; between came preliminary drawings, discarded
  lithography trials, two Paris trips, sculpture studies, and the 1907
  Villa Romana stay (prize 1906, founded by Klinger 1905). One
  breakthrough plate, then a slow commissioned build with heavy
  reworking, 1904-1908.
- The 1898 Kaiser veto is the documented prize incident: the jury
  voted her Weberaufstand cycle a gold medal, Wilhelm II blocked it
  ("orders and medals of honour belong on the breasts of worthy men").
  No separate 1902-03 Bauernkrieg controversy found; that conflation
  is flagged.
- The canonical seven in 1908 publication order: 1. Die Pflueger
  (The Plowmen), 2. Vergewaltigt (Raped), 3. Beim Dengeln
  (Sharpening the Scythe), 4. Bewaffnung im Gewaelbe (Arming in the
  Vault), 5. Losbruch (Outbreak, shown in English as Charge), 6.
  Feldschlacht (Battle Field), 7. Die Gefangenen (The Prisoners).
  Wikipedia's list swaps the last two; the plate-numbered museum
  records support this order. Creation order differs sharply:
  Losbruch first (1902/03), Dengeln per Harvard 1905 (final state
  1908), Arming 1906, Plowmen 1907, Battlefield 1907, Raped 1907/08,
  Prisoners 1908. The British Museum note: the Prisoners was not
  originally planned and became the deliberate closing plate.
- Edition: Emil Richter, Dresden, 1921, 200 copies, each sheet
  inscribed in the plate "1921 / Originalradierung Kaethe Kollwitz."
  Printer: Otto Felsing, Berlin, signed impressions by both artist
  and printer, early sheets printed "Druck v. O. Felsing." The same
  Felsing who printed Dix's Der Krieg: the lineage shares a press.
  No Delaetre connection; Eugène Delaatre was Paris and
  undocumented with her.
- Technique: drawing-to-plate via Ziegler's transfer paper, many
  preparatory drawings, trial proofs overworked in charcoal/ink to
  plan changes on the copper, many states (Losbruch XIII, Gefangenen
  X, Vergewaltigt VI, Dengeln XI). Combined intaglio vocabulary:
  line etching, drypoint (the furious linework of the Charge),
  aquatint for tonal masses, soft ground for drawing-like line, lift
  ground (Reservage) for reserved whites and sharp separations, plus
  sandpaper, emery, and textile impressions (fabric, ribbed laid
  paper) pressed into the soft ground. Tone controlled by aquatint
  depth and selective wiping; lights reserved by lift ground, not
  stopped out alone.
- Criticism: Getty's Marchesano exhibition (Prints, Process, Politics,
  2019-20) is the core treatment: Black Anna as the igniting center,
  drypoint as the energy system not decoration, fabric patterns as
  texture and class signifier, and Kollwitz's own verdict to Max
  Lehrs, "I consider this print to be my best work." MoMA's
  Asserting Herself (2024, Starr Figura): the Dengeln genesis as the
  star case, early versions a man with a downcast peasant, revisions
  ending in the solitary woman commanding the scythe, and her credo
  that she never did anything cold, always worked "with my blood."
  Hyperallergic places her "in the lineage of Goya's nightmarish
  Disasters of War," a critic's claim, no artist-stated link found.
  The MDPI Arts 2020 article: the best reading of Vergewaltigt, the
  only plate on an individual body's aftermath, the diagonal corpse,
  the face denied to preserve anonymity, the small hidden child.
  Craft consensus: technically above the Weberaufstand, "her highest
  achievements as an etcher."
- Lineage: no artist-stated Kollwitz link to Goya, Callot, or Duerer
  found. Dix modeled Der Krieg on Goya (Basel viewing, Herberholz
  retooling) with no documented statement crediting Kollwitz either
  way; what is documented is proximity, 1924-25: Dix accepted Ernst
  Friedrich's invitation to show Der Krieg alongside her Krieg
  woodcuts and Friedrich's photographs, and both hung in Friedrich's
  International War Museum in 1925. Treat "she influenced Dix" as
  unverified.
- Related: Aufruhr (Uprising) 1899, Klipstein 44, the discarded first
  idea with an allegorical nude Revolution and torch in the sky,
  replaced by the de-allegorized 1902 Losbruch.

## The seven plates

All seven visually inspected at 928-1400px from Wikimedia Commons
(public domain, source files listed with their Kn captions; the
impressions appear to be Richter editions, sheet 3 signed in the
image "Druck von O. Felsing, Berlin S.W."). Observations mine:

1. Die Pflueger (The Plowmen), 1907, Kn 99 VIII b. A woman harnessed
   to a plow, bent nearly double, pulling left, the plow wheel at
   right. The composition in three bands: worked gray sky above, a
   pale untouched band at the horizon, dark worked field below. The
   horizon band is reserved paper doing the separating; her shoulder
   and back are pale reserve against the dark field.
2. Vergewaltigt (Raped), 1907/08, Kn 101 V a. A woman's corpse
   sprawled diagonally in dense vegetation, the pale knee the one
   bright incident, the face denied. The MDPI reading confirmed in
   the pixels: the plant marks are a dark scribble field, the body
   reads by reserve, and the face is simply not there to meet the
   eye.
3. Beim Dengeln (Sharpening the Scythe), 1908, Kn 88 X b. The
   tightest crop in the cycle: face and hands filling a near-square
   sheet (1400x1414), the head cut by the top edge, sharpening the
   scythe with a whetstone. Pale face and hands against the dark
   surround; one small dark dot on the sleeve is the whole
   punctuation. The low Kn number against the late date and the X b
   state says this plate was begun early and reworked for years.
4. Bewaffnung im Gewaelbe (Arming in the Vault), 1906, Kn 96 VI,
   bicolour etching. The cycle's only vertical plate (928x1400), and
   its darkest. Peasants rising in a dark vault interior, raised
   scythes and forks, a lit arch at lower left, the crowd massed on
   the right two-thirds, the top half near-black. The crowd is still
   inside the dark here; it has not come out yet.
5. Losbruch (Charge), 1902/03, Kn 70 VIII b. The lightest plate by
   far. Black Anna at left, arms raised, the crowd surging left as
   one diagonal body, drawn almost entirely in line on pale reserve,
   the dark hill mass behind at right, pale sky above. The crowd is
   individual faces and strokes, never a fused mass; the unanimous
   direction is the picture.
6. Feldschlacht (Battle Field), 1907, Kn 100 X b. Night field,
   near-black, the largest plate of the cycle (41.3 x 69.2 cm per
   the AGO record). A bent figure with a lantern over the fallen;
   the lantern light the one pale incident, corpses drawn out of the
   dark by a few lines. The aftermath read by the searcher, not the
   battle.
7. Die Gefangenen (The Prisoners), 1908, Kn 102 IX a. The roped
   column: a horizontal frieze of bound men, standing in a row, the
   rope the load-bearing horizontal, every face individually worked.
   The only plate where the crowd stands still. The late deliberate
   closer: the cycle refuses a triumphant ending and ends on the
   inventory row.

The arc is composed: a four-plate descent into darkness (the vault
interior at the nadir, dark fraction 0.766, the only vertical plate),
then an explosive release into the lightest plate (the Charge, the
only plate drawn almost entirely in line on reserve), then the night
field aftermath, then the mid-light inventory close. The narrative
is told in light: burden, aftermath, preparation, the dark interior,
the revolt in full light, the dead field, the roll call. The two
darkest plates are the most compressed (std 26.9 and 22.4); the dark
core and the night field are held tight, not shaded. Note this is the
third serial value arc measured in the lineage run (Caprichos:
descent to plate 64 at 77.9 and dawn release; Der Krieg: three darks
then the pale relief valve; Bauernkrieg: the dark core then the line
explosion), and each works a different grammar.

## Re-render families

Family A: the series value strip. A1 published order, A2 shuffled
control, A3 chronological creation order (Charge 1902/03, Scythe
Harvard 1905/final 1908, Arming 1906, Plowmen 1907, Battle Field
1907, Raped 1907/08, Prisoners 1908), A4 reversed. A1 is the
species: descent, dark core, explosive release, aftermath, close.
A2 shuffled is noise, the pressure points misplaced. A3
chronological reads as a making-of portfolio: the Charge as an
opening bid, not a release, which kills the book. A4 reversed makes
the Charge a prologue, which it is not; the Prisoners end up in the
middle where they have nothing to close. Findings: the series is
composed in publication order; the dark core's verticality is
load-bearing (the only plate turned on its side); and the release
frame is drawn in the opposite technique, not just a higher value:
tone explodes into line.

Family B: the unanimous surge grammar of the Charge, rebuilt as
schematics. B1: unanimous diagonal, line-only bodies on pale
reserve, one lead figure with raised arms, one dark hill mass
behind. B2 control: same crowd as full-tone masses. B3 control:
mixed lean directions. B4 control: single centered figure. B1 is
the species: it reads as motion in one direction. B2 reads as a
queue, the tone domesticates the charge into a lineup. B3 reads as
milling, the unanimity was the energy. B4 is a stamp, an emblem.
Findings: unanimity is the composition, and the line-on-reserve is
what the crowd is made of; fill the masses in tone and the surge
dies. This is the counter-species to the Beckmann study's finding
(2026-10-05): Beckmann knots the crowd static in a stuffed frame,
Kollwitz surges it unanimous. Two species of the same instrument,
the composed crowd.

## Quality notes

- The value key is the narrative. The dossier's "narrative arc
  without heroes" is visible in the numbers: the series descends
  into the dark vault, explodes into the revolt in full light, and
  closes on the prisoners. A derivative piece would fail by
  shuffling this or by making every frame equally dark.
- Orientation is a compositional parameter. The dark core is the
  only vertical plate; the close-up is the only near-square; the
  night field is the widest sheet. The cycle changes shape where it
  changes state.
- The release frame changes technique, not just value. The Charge
  is the only plate with almost no worked ground: no aquatint sky,
  no sandpaper field, line and drypoint on reserve. After four
  plates of gathering tone, the drawing itself is the release.
- One igniting figure, one surging mass. Black Anna's raised arms
  are the vector of the whole picture; every other body leans the
  same way. The Getty finding confirmed: the drypoint linework is
  the energy system, not decoration.
- Agency transfer through revision. MoMA's Dengeln case: early
  versions a man with a downcast peasant, final state the solitary
  woman commanding the scythe. The quality decision was subtraction
  and re-centering across eleven states. A derivative would fail by
  adding figures instead of removing them.
- Denial of the face. In Vergewaltigt the face is withheld and the
  diagonal corpse plus the hidden child do the work; making the
  victim perform grief for the viewer is the named failure.
- Texture as class truth. The fabric and laid-paper imprints in the
  clothing are not ornament; they materialize peasant life at the
  grain level. A derivative would fail by using texture as a
  filter over forms that do not need it.
- The deliberate closer. The Prisoners was not originally planned;
  closing on the roped inventory row instead of the revolt is the
  compositional decision that refuses the triumphant reading. A
  derivative would fail by ending on the charge.
- The Felsing link. The same Berlin printer pulled Kollwitz's cycle
  and Dix's Der Krieg. The war-print lineage shares a press, which
  is worth more than the unverified influence claims.
- Honest about the lineage: no artist-stated Kollwitz debt to Goya,
  Callot, or Duerer was found, and none from Dix to her. The
  compositional rhymes are real (the unanimous surge, the dark
  interior before the revolt, the aftermath refusal of narrative)
  but the lineage is constructed by critics and by this study, not
  declared by the artists. Say so.

## Avoid list

- Pastoral peasant scenes without the tonal architecture (the
  content is not the peasant, the value arc is).
- The evenly spaced crowd inventory; mixed-direction crowds; the
  crowd rendered as full-tone masses (the B2 and B3 failures).
- The centered hero; the charge drawn in tone instead of line.
- Horror signifiers without the value discipline; the sentimental
  or maternal reading as the subject; the tortured-widow biography
  as branding (the Emory caution in the dossier).
- Allegory: her own 1899 Aufruhr with the torch-bearing nude
  Revolution is the documented dead end; she de-allegorized it into
  Losbruch.
- Even gradient washes as atmosphere; vignettes as darkness.
- Claiming the artist-stated Goya/Callot/Duerer links, or a
  documented Kollwitz-to-Dix debt, without the qualifiers above.
- "Zum Sturm!" as a Bauernkrieg plate; Frauenkopf as a cycle
  plate; Die Toten 1900/01 in the cycle (all flagged by the
  dossier: none exist as cycle plates).

## Honest gaps

- No original handled. All seven plates inspected at 928-1400px
  from Commons reproductions; tone curves are approximate, sheet 4
  at only 928px wide.
- The XIII-state reworking of Losbruch not traced state by state;
  the trial proofs overworked in charcoal not inspected.
- Dossier gaps carried: Plowmen date single-sourced (1907), Arming
  date unconfirmed, Dengeln's Harvard 1905 vs Commons 1908 dates
  unreconciled (low Kn 88 against late date reads as early start,
  long rework; not verified), 1908 exhibition reception of the
  finished cycle not found, Simplicissimus coverage of the cycle
  unconfirmed, no artist-stated links to Goya/Callot/Duerer, no
  Dix/Kollwitz statement either way, the alleged 1927-28 US trip
  unverified, Zimmermann readership by Kollwitz unverified.
- The 1902 Secession reviews of the Charge proof read only via the
  Getty's summary.
- The 1899 Aufruhr not visually inspected (described from the Hood
  Museum record via the dossier).
- Re-render families single pass; B schematics are crude strokes,
  the grammar not the drawing.
