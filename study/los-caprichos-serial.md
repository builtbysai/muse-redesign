# Los Caprichos as a serial study: the composed sequence (1799)

Deep study, 2026-10-05. Hop-only session, queue exhausted. Named as a
next doorway by the 2026-10-05 Velazquez study ("the Caprichos as a
serial study"), and earlier by the 2026-10-05 Goya Black Paintings study
and the 2026-10-04 Goya etcher study. The quality-first choice after the
Velazquez study closed the Spanish painterly run: the individual plates
were covered in the etcher study (plate 43, the Sleep of Reason, plus
Desastres, Tauromaquia, and two Disparates plates), but the SERIES as a
composed sequence had never been studied. A print series is not a set of
pictures. It is one picture in eighty frames, and the composition lives
in the pacing: what darkens, what releases, and where the hinge sits.

## Sources

Biography, publication, and scholarship via a delegated research dossier
(text-only pass; this study's visual inspections are mine), integrated
after the first push. Key facts, carried:

- The series was first conceived as "Suenos" (Dreams), after Quevedo's
  satirical dream-visions; at least 28 preparatory drawings survive, the
  drawings show moistening for transfer to the copper, and all but three
  of the dream designs carried over when Goya retitled it Caprichos. The
  definitive 80-print form was finished by 17 January 1799 (Osuna
  Archive receipt), announced 6 February 1799 in the Diario de Madrid
  (320 reales the set, sold at the Calle del Desengano No. 1 perfume
  shop, the ad's literary polish probably a friend's hand), 300 copies
  printed, 27 sold, withdrawn after about fourteen days (Goya's own 1803
  letter says two days, incorrectly), the copper plates and 240 unsold
  sets given to the King in 1803 for the Royal Chalcography in exchange
  for a lifetime pension for his son Javier. First-edition ink was
  slightly reddish or sepia; later Calcografia editions wore the
  aquatints down. Plate size holds at about 21.5 x 15 cm across the
  series.
- Technique: etching plus aquatint used together, which was new. Aquatint
  for the backgrounds and tonal modeling, highlights lifted with the
  burnisher scraping back toward white, drypoint and burin touches per
  plate. Goya drew first on paper, meticulously, then simplified and
  lightened in transfer (Lemoisne's Rembrandt comparison: the etchings
  read brighter than the drawings). The light is deliberately
  artificial: deep aquatint blacks that make the lit passages flare, the
  gray tones graded figure by figure for different emotional
  temperatures inside one plate.
- The scholarship's working division: plates 1 to 3 as prologue (the
  programmatic self-portrait, the marriage satire's opening, the
  bogeyman announcing the register of fear); the first-half satires
  (marriage, education, the Inquisition plates 23-26, the tight asses
  run 37-42); plate 43 as the axis, originally designed as the
  frontispiece ("Suenos, the first universal language," the Author
  Dreaming), moved to the midpoint instead: the first half satirizes
  from reason's point of view, the second half follows dream logic;
  then the witchcraft sequence (44, 45, 59, 61, 62, 64-69, 71) and the
  duendes group in parallel (46, 49, 52, 70, 74, 79, 80), where "duende"
  was slang for friar, which is why the goblins wear habits. The finale
  is plates 79-80: the Met reads "Ya es hora" two ways, it is time for
  the night creatures to wake, or it is time for their actions to be
  exposed.
- Plate-to-plate mechanics, documented: the 19-20-21 chain on
  prostitution (19 sets the lure, 20 shows the plucked, 21 reverses it
  and the prostitute is plucked by law, Hughes's reading); the 64/65
  flight pair (the night flight, then the mother flying off to join
  it); the 42/63 long echo (42: peasants carrying the idle as donkeys,
  the world upside down but reformable; 63: the peasants themselves
  donkey-like, the riders monsters, no hope left). Whether the looser
  overall ordering is real or protective camouflage against the censor
  is widely repeated but thinly sourced; it sits uneasily beside these
  deliberate local pairings.
- The captions: Goya's own engraved titles under each plate, brief and
  aphoristic, doing half the satirical work. Around publication,
  handwritten commentaries circulated: the Prado manuscript (cautious,
  defusing, possibly a smokescreen), the Ayala manuscript (naming names,
  anticlerical), the Biblioteca Nacional manuscript (published by
  Helman, 1951). The three agree and diverge plate by plate; on 55 the
  Prado is flat while Ayala names the Duchess of Osuna and others see
  the queen.
- Conflicts carried: 1803 vs 1807 for the plate gift (1803 per the body
  sources and the Calcografia shop, working date); fourteen days vs two
  days on sale; thirteen official editions vs twenty evidenced; the
  protective-disorder theory thinly sourced; Helman's Moratin witchcraft
  source doubted on chronology though the visual coincidences stand;
  "sueno" meaning both sleep and dream, so the monsters are either what
  happens when reason sleeps or what reason itself dreams.

## The six plates

Plate 1, Self-portrait (334137, DP816886, 2533x3727). The series opens
with the author's face, not a satire. Profile bust, the tall top hat the
darkest mass in the plate, the face fully reserved, the ground one flat
mid aquatint with edge hatching. The plate sits small on a huge sheet,
"P. 1" top right, the script title "Fran.co Goya y Lucientes, Pintor"
below the platemark. The address comes first: a face, a name, a trade.
Every plate after this is signed by the man in the hat.

Plate 20, Ya van desplumados (380474, DP811830, 2703x3729). The plucked
birds walk off to the right, the women sweep them along with a broom,
the bird of prey hangs top right (the lure from plate 19, Todos caeran:
the trap and the consequence, taught as a pair). A pale reserved
rectangle at left holds a dog-like bird. Light value key overall, the
action confined to the bottom half, the top half aquatint sky doing the
waiting. The sequence teaches by adjacency: 19 sets the lure, 20 shows
the plucked.

Plate 50, Los Chinchillas (380464, DP816989, 2638x3647). Two muzzled
figures in white coats, fully reserved pale masses against a near-black
aquatint ground, a dark servant spoon-feeding one of them, the basket
between them. The muzzles (the locks on the heads) are the small pale
accents that name the picture. One of the darkest plates of the first
half; the white coats are the brightest event, and the joke needs the
dark: privilege as a white shape on a black ground.

Plate 56, Subir y bajar (334183, DP817116, 2667x3789). The favorite
lifted by goblins, the fallen figure below, the composition one diagonal
exchange: up and down in the same frame. The lifted figure pale against
the mid-dark ground, the fallen one's pale back the brightest mass. The
title is the composition: rise and fall are one gesture, and the plate
draws both at once.

Plate 64, Buen viage (334184, DP817120, 2493x3617). The night flight.
Full-bleed near-black aquatint, the darkest of the six (mean 77.9), the
bat-demon carrying its witches, only the faces and one shoulder reserved
pale, the bottom third nearly empty dark ground. The incident cluster
sits high, the void falls below: this is the species of the witch
sequence, the composed dark the etcher study measured plate by plate,
now seen as the sequence's lowest point.

Plate 80, Ya es hora (380711, DP817135, 2748x3722). The finale, and the
lightest of the six (mean 165.5). The monks rise from their coffins at
dawn, yawning, pale on pale, the gestures on diagonals. The series ends
in light: the sleepers wake. After the witch dark, the release is a
value decision, not just a subject decision. Goya closes the book by
returning the key he opened it in.

## Tonal arc

Plate-area means (fixed crop, verified by overlay):

- Plate 1: 165.2, std 62.3 (widest range: the hat black against the reserved face)
- Plate 20: 158.1, std 46.3
- Plate 50: 143.3, std 56.2
- Plate 56: 109.1, std 39.2
- Plate 64: 77.9, std 22.1 (darkest, most compressed: the night flight)
- Plate 80: 165.5, std 46.7 (the finale returns to the prologue's key)

The value key is free per plate, exactly as every study in this lineage
has found, but across the series it is an arc: the light prologue, the
satire mids, the dark descent through the witch sequence with its lowest
point at the night flight, and the dawn release. Plate 43 (the Sleep of
Reason, covered in the etcher study) is the axis the descent pivots on.

## Re-render families

Family A, the series arc (80-cell value strip, anchors 1/20/50/56/64/80
measured, the rest interpolated between anchors). A1, the composed arc:
descent after the hinge marker at 43, lowest at the night flight,
release at the finale. A2, the same values shuffled: noise, no pacing,
eighty pictures instead of one series. A3, flat mid value with jitter:
an edition, not a series, every frame interchangeable. A4, the inverted
arc (dark ends, light middle): the wrong pacing, horror first and satire
last, the book read backwards. Finding: the sequence is paced as a
descent and a release; the hinge at 43 is load-bearing; shuffle the order
and the composition is gone even though no plate changed.

Family B, the hinge grammar (plate 43 as schematic: the sleeper, the
dream, the title). B1, the species: the horizontal split, the sleeper's
head a reserved pale mass on the desk below, the dark dream field above
with the creature clusters, the title lozenge written inside the dark
(the desk carries the words in the real plate). B2, creatures spread over
the whole field including the sleeper: the dream without the dreamer,
noise. B3, the sleeper alone on the pale field: a study of a sleeper,
no hinge. B4, the split on the vertical axis: two unrelated panels, the
dream beside the dreamer instead of above him. Finding: the hinge works
because the dreamer and the dream share one frame on the horizontal
axis, sleep below and monsters above; the title inside the picture makes
the plate self-describing, the caption and the image one object.

## Quality notes

What makes the series good is the pacing, not the plates. Any single
Capricho is a fine etching; the eighty together are a composition, and
the composition is a descent with a release. The prologue is an address
(a face, a name, a trade), the hinge is a turn (the sleeper divides the
book: satire before, dream after), the finale is a key change (the
lightest plate closes the darkest sequence). The captions are the second
half of each picture: the script title under the plate is not a label,
it is the punchline's timing, the Daumier finding repeated (his captions
were the second half of the lithograph too). The series teaches by
adjacent contrast: 19 sets the lure and 20 shows the plucked; the pair
is the unit, not the plate. And the value arc is the series' real
narrative: Goya tells the story in light, descending into the witch dark
and surfacing at dawn, which is why the finale had to be the lightest
plate. A derivative series fails when it shuffles: same plates, no arc,
no book.

## Avoid list

- The monster without the hinge: witches and goblins as spooky
  signifiers with no series arc behind them.
- The shuffled series: strong plates in a random order, which is a
  portfolio, not a composition.
- The flat series: every frame in the same value key, no descent, no
  release.
- Captions as decoration: titles that label instead of completing the
  picture.
- The self-portrait treated as just another plate: the prologue is an
  address, not a satire.
- Horror as the genre: the witch sequence is the middle of an arc, not
  the point of the book.
