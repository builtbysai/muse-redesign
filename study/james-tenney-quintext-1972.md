## The suite, stated plainly

Quintext is Five Textures for String Quartet and Bass, 1972, about 30
minutes on the score (35 on the Bozzini recording). Five movements, each a
texture dedicated to a fellow composer, each built by a different method,
each a homage that could never be mistaken for the dedicatee's own music:

- I. Some Recent THOUGHTS for Morton Feldman: soft, sustained,
  nondevelopmental, dissonant vertical harmonies, the obsession of Feldman's
  early work (the Vertical Thoughts series, which gave the movement its
  title). But the harmonies are microtonal, in just intonation on a scale
  analogous to the first 13 odd-number harmonics, Tenney's own obsession and
  a concept totally at odds with Feldman's devotion to equal temperament.
- II. CLOUDS for Iannis Xenakis: a tapestry of sound and silence, the sound
  sections a homage to the string cluster textures of Xenakis's Metastasis
  and other works, but randomly derived.
- III. A Choir of ANGELS for Carl Ruggles: a textural parody of Ruggles's
  Angels with actual quotations of chords from Ruggles's score, retuned to
  just intonation, played sul ponticello throughout to invoke the muted brass
  of the original.
- IV. PARABOLAS and HYPERBOLAS for Edgard Varese: continual but irregular
  glissandi finally converging around middle C, evoking the parabolic and
  hyperbolic shapes Varese wanted to create in sound with instruments like
  the siren; graphic notation, and a degree of randomness almost antithetical
  to Varese's own methods.
- V. SPECTRA for Harry Partch: nine minutes, the most unprecedented of the
  group, an ingenious scordatura that reaches the 105th harmonic of F1.

That is the suite's real doctrine: one suite, five incompatible methods,
each movement a complete portrait of a different way of organizing sound.
Feldman gets verticality, Xenakis gets clouds, Ruggles gets quotation,
Varese gets curves, Partch gets the harmonic series itself.

## Quintet V: the technique, in full

The contrabass tunes its IV (E) string up to F1, of which it then plays
select natural harmonics up to the 15th so that the other instruments can
tune their open strings to these. The bass plays only its "F" string
throughout, but its other strings are tuned to harmonics of this pitch so
that they resonate sympathetically with it; the cello's IV string is
similarly treated as a resonator. All pitches sounded are either open
strings or natural harmonics up to the 7th, so the highest harmonic
available is number 7 x 15 = 105 on the first violin's I string. Tenney
notates all pitches as they sound, borrowing the artificial-harmonic
convention for natural harmonics: sounding pitch with a figure in
parentheses indicating the string (filled notehead), and the touched node
with an open diamond notehead. The score uses proportional time notation
but is fully determinate.

The form is three parts. The opening section, about 60 seconds: the bass's
mezzo forte F1 drone begins and holds through most of the piece. An
additive-synthesis process unfolds over the next half minute as successively
higher harmonics of the drone (all sustained on open strings) enter with
gradually increasing rapidity in the order {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 11, 13,
15}, although some octave equivalents are subsequently deleted, so the
opening culminates with the sounding harmonic set {1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13,
15}. The members of this set then drop out in high-to-low order until the
60-second mark, when F1 sounds alone. Wannamaker calls this an "exposition"
of the open strings, the exploration of their natural harmonics that
follows being the "development."

The main section: a gradual introduction of successively higher harmonics
of the F1 drone. Players sustain each notated pitch until a new one is
indicated. The score's dynamic instruction is the piece's perceptual core:
tones other than the mezzo forte bass drone should be very soft, "hovering
near some threshold between being heard as individual tones at all, on the
one hand, and being heard simply as intensifications of some harmonic in the
spectrum of the bass's low F." Over the next 50 seconds all pitches
obtainable using only open strings are heard, in an apparently stochastic
order. Then the set is enlarged by adding the first harmonics of open
strings, with successively higher harmonics added in stages, until the
gamut reaches the 7th natural harmonics at roughly the golden section of
the movement's duration (5'30"). The attack timepoints undergo a continuous,
roughly exponential accelerando from about 5 seconds per attack to 0.83
seconds per attack, then hold constant to the end of the section. Nearing
the section's end, lower harmonics of open strings disappear until only
open-string harmonics 5, 6, and 7 remain (7'00" to 7'50"). The bass drone
skips upward to natural harmonics: first F2, then F3 for the last 30
seconds. The "fundamental" thus becomes virtual, since not all sounding
tones are harmonics of these new bass pitches.

The conclusion: the instruments begin sustaining harmonics 1, 2, 3, 5, 7,
9, 11, 13, and 15 of F3, the cumulative verticality of the opening section
transposed up two octaves, taken up in an irregularly descending order, some
as double stops. Once the full verticality sounds, its tones begin a
sequence of downward octave transfers in strict sequential order from
highest harmonic number (15) to lowest (1). After the first set, the
complete verticality is heard above fundamental F2; a second set of octave
transfers returns the piece to the verticality over F1 heard in the opening
section. Finally, in a retrogression of the movement's opening process,
pitches drop out starting with the highest harmonic numbers until only the
fading F1 drone remains.

## What makes it sing

The scordatura is a tuning machine, not a tuning: the player never learns
rational arithmetic, the acoustics of the instrument take care of that
(Polansky's phrase, borrowed from this piece for his own Movement for Lou
Harrison). One retuned string times natural harmonics yields 105 partials
of exact just intonation from players who only have to touch nodes and bow.
That is the same economy Clang wanted from its orchestra (players bending
pitches "a little bit in certain directions") pushed to its limit: here the
limitation of the mechanism is the source of the precision.

The threshold dynamic is the composition. "Hovering near some threshold
between being heard as individual tones at all, on the one hand, and being
heard simply as intensifications of some harmonic in the spectrum of the
bass's low F": the whole nine minutes is a meditation on one perceptual
boundary, the line where harmony becomes timbre. Wannamaker's summary is
worth quoting whole: "Tones in QUINTEXT V lead double lives, alternately as
contributors to the timbre of a quasi-steady-state spectrum that subsumes
them and as autonomous percepts available to participate in harmonic
relationships... When they are discernible, one might regard the role of
the string quartet's tones as illuminatory, subtly highlighting first one
then another of the components and relationships latent within the bass's
tone (or, more precisely, within our perception of that tone). The
representation of very high partials not otherwise discriminable, however,
seems to go beyond this in a gesture towards the preternatural and the
transcendence of acoustical and perceptual horizons."

The form is an arch with a trapdoor. The conclusion restates the opening's
verticality two octaves up and walks it back down by octave transfers, and
the walk is strict where the accumulation was irregular: irregularity
builds the chord, strictness dissolves it. The golden section governs twice:
the ceiling expansion completes at 5'30", and the accelerando plateaus
there too. And the "virtual fundamental" at the end of the main section,
the bass skipping to F2 and F3 while the upper tones stop being its
harmonics, is a quiet joke about the whole spectralist premise: the
fundamental is a perception, not a string.

What is overdone, by Tenney's own later standard: nothing here. Gilmore
calls it "practically a textbook of Tenney techniques," and the piece
earned that by being the first place where the scordatura engine, the
threshold dynamic, and the harmonic-series form all appear together. The
avoid-list note is narrower: the homage suite as a format risks reading as
pastiche, and Tenney dodges it only because each movement's method is
genuinely his, not the dedicatee's. A portrait that uses the subject's
palette is fan art; a portrait that uses your own palette is a portrait.
Quintext is the second kind, five times.

## Relation to Clang and the Bell Labs circle

Clang (same year, same harmonic-series material) is the public,
orchestral, indeterminate version of the idea; Quintext V is the private,
chamber, fully determinate one. Clang's available-pitch process picks
pitches at random from prime-partial sets and asks players to swell and
fade; Quintext V's main section picks open-string pitches in an apparently
stochastic order and asks players to sustain until replaced, hovering at
the audibility threshold. Both end by walking the harmonic ladder
downward: Clang's dissolutive process weeds non-harmonics against an
ascending conceptual fundamental; Quintext V's retrogression drops
harmonics high-to-low against the fading F1 drone. The two pieces are the
same thought in opposite social forms: one for an orchestra that cannot be
precisely tuned, one for five strings that can.

## For the generative practice

- The threshold dynamic is directly portable: tones that hover between
  being heard as tones and being heard as brightening of a drone. A visual
  analog: marks that hover between being seen as marks and being seen as
  texture in a field. The interest is all in the boundary, and the boundary
  is a parameter, not a state.
- The scordatura trick: a tiny retuning of the instrument (one string up a
  semitone) times a simple playing rule (touch nodes) yields a large exact
  structure (105 just partials). The generative equivalent: retune the
  coordinate system slightly, then apply the dumbest possible rule inside
  it. Precision from mechanism, not from control.
- Five textures, five methods: a suite where each movement re-derives its
  construction from scratch. The discipline for a series: never reuse the
  engine, reuse only the frame (five players, one dedication each).
