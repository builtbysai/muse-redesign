# Los Disparates plate by plate: the unarcable series (1815-1823)

Deep study, 2026-10-05. Hop-only session, queue exhausted. Named as an
open doorway by the 2026-10-05 Kollwitz, Dix, Beckmann, Caprichos, and
Velazquez studies, and earlier by the 2026-10-04 and 2026-10-05 Goya
studies. The quality-first choice: Goya's last print series, the one the
etcher study covered only at two plates (Volante and Cruel), and the
series the lineage has been circling since Rembrandt's states and
Segers's reworked plates. Twenty-two surviving plates, the darkest set of
his four great series, never published in his lifetime. The finding of
this study is that the Disparates are a collection, not a sequence: the
composed arc the Caprichos and Dix and Kollwitz studies measured is
absent here, and the absence is the composition. The derivative lesson
is what a series looks like when it refuses pacing.

## Sources

Facts via a delegated research dossier (text-only pass; the six visual
inspections and both re-render families are mine). Key facts, carried:

- The series was etched at the Quinta del Sordo, left apparently
  incomplete when Goya moved to Bordeaux in 1824. Twenty-two copper
  plates survive; the highest number on his state proofs is 25, so
  twenty-five were likely planned. Goya never titled the series, never
  published it, and left no statement of purpose: the 1863 "Suenos"
  label and the 1864 "Proverbios" framing are other people's
  inventions. The accepted title comes from Goya's own handwritten
  proof epigraphs ("Disparate femenino," "Disparate volante," ...),
  which surfaced in the early twentieth century; even in 1971 Sayre
  still published on them as "Late Caprichos."
- Technique: etching and aquatint, retouched with drypoint, burin,
  lavis, and burnishing in different combinations per plate. The dark
  grounds are burnished aquatint: worked black, not inked black. All
  plates are horizontal, platemark roughly 24.2 to 24.5 by 35.1 to
  35.5 cm.
- Publication: the 22 plates stayed with his heirs. In 1862 the Real
  Academia de Bellas Artes de San Fernando acquired 18 of them and
  published them in 1864 as Los Proverbios, edition of 360, in an order
  scholars agree was random, with publisher-assigned titles. The other
  four (the toritos, bestia, conocido, puntual plates) were published
  in the Paris magazine L'Art in 1877; their copper plates entered the
  Louvre in 2011.
- The titling dispute is real and documented: plate 6 is Disparate cruel
  in the Prado/English convention and Disparate furioso in the Spanish
  Wikipedia, MFA Houston, and Camon Aznar; plate 7 is Disparate
  desordenado vs Disparate matrimonial. Which form is in Goya's hand is
  unverified from accessible sources, so the note uses Cruel and
  desordenado with the alternates carried.
- Scholarship: no consensus on a composed sequence. Jesusa Vega's
  1996 Calcografia catalogue treats it as a collection of 22 plates.
  Glendinning's carnival reading (subversion of authority, marriage,
  army, clergy) is the strongest thematic thesis; Tomlinson reads the
  series as the triumph of caprice and a departure continued in the
  Black Paintings; Sayre's authority is process, the drawing-to-print
  development. Yriarte and others linked it to the Caprichos and the
  emphatic Desastres plates. Bozal and Vega date the work 1815/16 to
  1819, possibly to 1823.
- Plate 6 connection CONFIRMED: the preparatory gouache shows a soldier
  expelling people with his rifle at a military sentry box; in the
  print the soldier has become a civilian, the sentry box is gone, and
  the rifle is a pike piercing a man's face. Both Wikipedia articles
  confirm it, citing the Prado drawing.
- The Cean Bermudez album (British Museum) places a state proof of
  plate 13 (Modo de volar) immediately after the last Tauromaquia
  plate, and the copper and paper stock match the Tauromaquia, so the
  first Disparates were made right after the Tauromaquia, 1815-16.

## The six plates

Plate 1, Disparate femenino (DP817496, 3804x2814). Five women around a
blanket, tossing a military-uniformed straw man (the manteo del
pelele) into the night air above them. The ground is banded: a dark
aquatint ceiling over a hatched middle foreground, the women's dresses
in pale reserve against both. The tossed figure is the incident, small,
high, pale; the composition's energy is the five women looking up at
what they threw. A satire of authority that opens with carnival
punishment, not with an address: there is no self-portrait prologue
here, no plate 43 hinge to come.

Plate 2, Disparate de miedo (DP817498, 3810x2744). The ghost disguise:
a towering figure in white sheets, the face peeking from a sleeve,
looming over a tree and a scatter of small panicking figures below.
The ghost is the one great pale vertical, nearly the full plate
height, worked in white reserve against the full-bleed dark. The
frightened figures are drawn in line and hatch, small, along the
bottom. The composition is a single scale shock: the giant white
incident over the small dark incident, the night doing the rest.

Plate 3, Disparate ridiculo (DP817487, 3822x2688). The counter-species:
figures tangled in a tree branch, floating in mid-air, against a flat
mid-gray aquatint field, the lowest mean of the early plates but the
lightest ground of the six. No night ceiling, no hatched foreground:
the field is one composed middle value, the tree the one dark diagonal
mass, the figures pale incidents caught in it. The flat field is what
makes it strange; the figures hang in a value that is neither sky nor
room.

Plate 10, El caballo raptor (DP817491, 3816x2669). The woman thrown
from the rearing horse, her body arched across the upper middle, a
second fallen figure at left, the horse massed dark at right. The
ground is full-bleed night with a pale center where the action is: the
darkest mean so far (122.3), the widest range of the six (57.6). The
reserve zone is composed, not found: the pale opens exactly around the
thrown body and the horse's pale chest, and closes to near-black at
every edge.

Plate 13, Modo de volar (DP817478, 3804x2661). Four winged flyers, men
with bat-like contraptions, on the near-black full-bleed ground. The
lead flyer is large at upper left in pale reserve, the other three
recede to lower right at descending scales: one instrument family at
four sizes. Darkest mean of the measured plates (100.9), dark fraction
0.523. Depth is carried entirely by scale recession; there is no
perspective, no horizon, no ground line. This is the species of the
late dark plates: one family of pale forms, sized, on night.

Plate 20, Disparate puntual (NGA 52384, 4000x2702). A woman standing on
a horse that stands on a tightrope, a crowd of faces massed behind in
hatch tone, the rope one taut line. The ground is full-bleed near-black
above and below, the horse and rider fully reserved, the crowd a dark
band across the middle. The lightest plate of the six to read (the
crowd hatch keeps it moving) but the darkest measured mean (86.1),
dark fraction 0.612: the black top and bottom thirds do the work. The
rope is the composition: a single straight line holding the whole
impossible stack.

## Tonal arc

Plate-area means (fixed center crops, verified by overlay; 1864
Proverbios impressions, so aquatints worn lighter than the first
printing):

- Plate 1 femenino: 142.6, std 49.7, dark fraction 0.036
- Plate 2 miedo: 141.3, std 47.7, dark fraction 0.027
- Plate 3 ridiculo: 134.8, std 49.7, dark fraction 0.143
- Plate 5 volante: 133.3 (2026-10-04 etcher study)
- Plate 6 cruel: 151.4 (etcher study, the palest of the early plates)
- Plate 10 caballo raptor: 122.3, std 57.6, dark fraction 0.330
- Plate 13 modo de volar: 100.9, std 52.0, dark fraction 0.523
- Plate 20 puntual: 86.1, std 39.9, dark fraction 0.612

The published order descends into night. The early plates work in
banded grounds (dark ceiling over hatched foreground) or mid fields;
from plate 10 the grounds go full-bleed and the dark fraction climbs
past one half. But the order is the 1864 publisher's, and the highest
proof number suggests the series was never finished: the descent may be
editorial accident. What is certain, plate by plate, is the technical
arc: from zoned grounds to the burnished full-bleed black, and the
figures' shift from drawn incident to reserved incident. The etcher
study's finding holds at series scale: the brightest value is
untouched ground, the light is subtracted.

## Re-render families

Family A, the 22-cell series strip (8 measured anchors, the rest
interpolated, single pass). A1, the published order: a steady descent
from 142.6 to 86.1, plates 19 to 22 extrapolated from plate 20. A2,
shuffled: noise, no pacing, twenty-two pictures. A3, value-sorted: a
smooth gradient, an edition not a series. A4, reversed: night first,
satire last. Finding: the published order does descend, but unlike the
Caprichos study there is no prologue plate, no hinge at a midpoint, no
release at the finale, and no documented Goya order at all. The A1
strip is a publisher's sequence that happens to darken; the
compositional arc is not in the plates, it is an artifact of the
ordering. A derivative series fails when it forces a hinge plate or a
release valve onto this species: the Disparates refuse arc, and the
refusal is the point of view.

Family B, the night grammar (Modo de volar as schematic: one family of
pale flyer glyphs). B1, the species: full-bleed dark ground, one family
of pale forms at receding scales, positions scattered. B2, same forms
at uniform size on a centered grid: inventory, a specimen chart, no
flight. B3, the vignette ground (dark center fading to paper at the
edges): a spotlight, not night; the paper edges kill the dark. B4, the
forms filled mid-tone instead of pale reserve: overcast, the incident
gone. Finding: the ground must reach every edge (the vignette is the
named failure), the brightest value must be the untouched reserve, and
depth comes from scale recession alone. Uniform scale makes a chart;
tone fill makes weather; only the receding pale family makes flight.

## Quality notes

What makes the series good is the per-plate refusal, not any sequence.
Every study in the print lineage found a serial composition: the
Caprichos' descent and release with the hinge at 43, Dix's folder
macro-structure with the pale relief valves, Kollwitz's tonal arc with
the dark core and the line explosion. The Disparates have none of that:
no prologue, no axis, no closer, no documented order, and the titles
are the publisher's. Yet the plates are as good as anything in the
lineage. The quality is in the single frame's night grammar: the
full-bleed burnished ground, the figure fully reserved, the scale
shock (plate 2's ghost, plate 20's tightrope stack), the scale
recession (plate 13's flyers). Goya at the end of his life stopped
composing sequences and composed nights: each plate is a complete
instrument, one ground, one incident family, nothing else. A derivative
series fails when it treats the plates as episodes needing a story, or
when it borrows the night as a filter: vignette darks, evenly worked
grounds, figures drawn on top of the black instead of reserved out of
it. The Cruel rework is the process thesis of the whole series: the
drawing showed a soldier with a rifle at a sentry box, and the print
shows a civilian with a pike. He took the specific authority out and
left the violence in. That is what the series does to everything: it
removes the address, the caption, the order, the prologue, and leaves
the night.

## Avoid list

- The forced arc: a hinge plate, a prologue address, a release valve
  imposed on a series whose species refuses pacing.
- The shuffled series read as a failure: here the shuffle is the
  honest form, the published order is the accident.
- Vignette or gradient darks as Goya-like night: the ground must go
  edge to edge, full-bleed.
- Figures drawn on the black instead of reserved from it: the brightest
  value is untouched ground.
- Uniform-scale figure families: inventory, not flight.
- Spooky signifiers without the value discipline: ghosts, giants, and
  flyers as horror decoration.
- The tortured-old-genius story as branding; the proverb reading as
  fact (the 1864 titles are the publisher's).
- "Giant in the landscape" and "Folly of youth" as plate references:
  unverified against the plate list (the dossier's closest match for
  the giant is an un-engraved drawing).
- Citing the 1864 edition size as 300 without the 360 counterpoint;
  citing Cruel or Furioso without the alternate.

## Honest gaps

No original handled. Six of twenty-two plates inspected; the arc strip
interpolates between eight measured anchors and plates 19 to 22 are
extrapolated from plate 20 (they were not even numbered in the 1864
edition: the four L'Art plates are lettered, not numbered). All six are
1864 Proverbios impressions, so the aquatints are worn lighter than
the first printing. The dossier's own gaps are carried: the 1864
individual proverb titles unverified against a primary list, the
Cruel/Furioso and desordenado/matrimonial splits unresolved against
Goya's handwriting, the 1875 second edition dealer-claimed only, no
Goya statement of purpose found anywhere, and the execution dating
spreads 1815-16 to 1819-1823 across sources. Plate 10's "gigantic rat"
landscape reading comes from the Spanish Wikipedia summary, not from
a named scholar in the dossier. Schematics single-pass; the Family B
glyphs are schematic flyers, not traced forms. Evidence in

tonal stats, dossier).
