# Erik Satie: Study Notes

**Profile:** Erik Satie (Honfleur, May 17, 1866, to Paris, July 1, 1925). Named as next doorway by the 2026-10-03 John Cage study (Cheap Imitation, the sympathetic source). The quality-first choice after the chance-protocol study: where Cage removed intention, Satie removed ambition. He composed as if music were a small object you could hold in one hand: brief pieces, sparse chords, absurd titles, and a second instrument in his handwriting. The study question was why his restraint reads as wit rather than poverty, and why a three-chord piece can feel like a complete thought.

**Depth:** deep on text, six artworks visually inspected at source resolution, two local re-render families built and inspected, pass 2 on both families (A1 motif scale lifted, field grid rebuilt from 30x28 to 40x21 with the boundary beat as a subtle value shift; B1 prose lines re-set shorter after a right-edge overflow). Read end to end: Wikipedia (biography, the Montmartre and Arcueil periods, the Schola Cantorum turn, Parade, Socrate, the writings, the furniture-music and Vexations passages); the Vexations article in full (the 840 inscription, the 1949/1958/1969 publication history, the 1963 Pocket Theatre marathon with Cage's time clock and 5-cent refunds, the meaning conjectures, the execution ambiguities); the Furniture music article in full (the five pieces in three sets, the 1920 Galerie Barbazanges premiere as intermission music, the manuscript manifesto "it isn't boring", the Cage revival); the Sports et divertissements article (the Vogel commission, the one-page-per-piece constraint, black ink for notes and words with red ink for staves, the India ink facsimile); the Cheap Imitation article (Cage's 1969 Socrate imitation through the three nested I Ching questions, the last work he performed in public, "I owe that confusion to love"). Visually inspected: the Vexations engraved score page (commons 650x515: the NOTE DE L'AUTEUR inscription, Tres lent, the 13-group bass theme with its dense enharmonic accidentals, the "A ce signe il sera d'usage de presenter le theme de la Basse" instruction); the Sports et divertissements Preface facsimile (1104x838: calligraphic prose across the top, "I put into it everything I know about Boredom", "I dedicate this Chorale to those who do not like me. I withdraw", then the Choral inappetissant on pale red staves with the "15 Mai 1914 (Le matin, a jeun)" date); the Le Tango (perpetuel) facsimile (1000x760: the same two-beat cell tiled across the whole page, prose threaded between systems, "Modere & tres ennuye", signed and dated); the Socrate manuscript title page (835x634: brown ink program notes flowing across red staves, words over the music paper, redactions); the Gymnopedie No. 3 engraved page (1787x819: bass notes F and B-flat low, the left-hand chord stabs alternating, the melody occupying tiny fractions of each bar). ## The core finding: smallness is the point of view

Every quality claim below reduces to one sentence. Satie never wrote more music than the idea needed, never wrote more words than the page needed, and never asked the listener for more attention than the piece deserved. The Gymnopedie is three chords and a breath. Vexations is thirteen chord groups and a count. The furniture pieces are five miniatures "to be played in a vestibule". The Sports pieces are one page each, never two. This is not minimalism as style. It is a refusal: of development, of the big Romantic gesture, of music that demands the room. A derivative that shrinks a conventional composition has the mechanism backwards. Satie starts from the small and refuses to leave it.

## Quality notes: what makes his work good, and what would make a derivative fail

1. **The count is the architecture.** Vexations is not a piece about repetition. It is a thirteen-chord motif, small enough to fit on half a page, plus the number 840 written above it in prose. The re-render proved it. The motif alone (A1) is a doodle, a study sheet. The motif plus the 840-mark field (A2), with every 40th mark carrying a subtle beat, is the species: the small object plus the count as a visible frame. The randomized field (A3) is static, decoration with no ceremony. The motif blown up and repeated four times large (A4) is a poster, and the duration collapses. A derivative that varies the motif to keep the listener interested has missed it. The motif never varies. The number is the composition, and the number is exact and kept.

2. **The page is the instrument.** In the Sports facsimiles, the music is the weakest element on the page and that is the design. The re-render proved the hierarchy. The species (B1): pale red staves as the ground, a handful of black chord events spaced wide, prose threaded inside the staff space, the calligraphic title, the date as a signature. The same notes on heavy black staves with no text (B2) die into a chord chart; the music alone is bland. Text dominating with a few notes (B3) becomes illustrated prose; the words stop being part of the music. A derivative that sets beautiful notes on a beautiful page and then captions it has the layers backwards. The words are not captions. They live between the staves, in the music's own air.

3. **The joke is a discipline, not a decoration.** "Modere & tres ennuye." "I put into it everything I know about Boredom." "In order to play the motif 840 times, prepare yourself in the deepest silence by serious immobilities." The humor is never pasted on after the fact. Each piece's title and instructions are the piece's thesis: the Le Tango is perpetual because the cell never changes; the Choral inappetissant warns off the serious-minded because the music is deliberately sour. A derivative that writes a serious miniature and adds a funny title has a gag. Satie's titles describe the mechanism. The instruction "arm yourself with clairvoyance" is the only honest way to play the chord that follows it.

4. **Write less, then refuse to add.** The furniture music manifesto says it plainly: "it won't wear out; it isn't boring." The Sports constraint (one page per piece) does the rest. The Tango page shows the practice: one two-beat cell, tiled. Not developed, not varied, not ornamented. The Gymnopedie shows it again: the bass note, the chord stab, the melody fragment, then rest. The white space is not empty; it is the piece refusing to continue. A derivative that fills the bar because the bar is there is doing the opposite. Satie's rests are composed. The silence between the chord and the next chord is doing the harmonic work.

5. **Unresolved is a home, not a problem.** The Vexations chords never resolve; the enharmonic spelling makes them hard to read as well as to hear. The Gymnopedie chords hang in the air with no functional progress. This is the taste move that lets him be sparse: if a chord must resolve, you need the next chord, and then the next, and the piece grows. If the chord is allowed to just sit there, three chords are a complete world. A derivative that adds a cadence "to land it" has destroyed the piece. The refusal to resolve is what keeps everything small.

6. **The date is part of the work.** "15 Mai 1914 (Le matin, a jeun)." "5 Mai 1914." Every Sports page is signed and dated, and the dates are doing compositional work: they anchor the joke, they mark the brevity (three weeks, twenty pieces), they tell the performer the piece was written before breakfast. A derivative that omits the provenance keeps the notes and loses the frame. The date says: this was one morning's work, and it is finished.

## Avoid-list additions

- Repetition with varied motifs presented as Satie-like (the motif never varies; the count is the composition).
- Serious miniatures with funny titles pasted on (the title must describe the mechanism).
- Beautiful scores with prose as caption (the words live inside the staff space, not above it).
- Filled bars "because the bar is there" (the rests are composed; refusal to continue is the piece).
- Added cadences to "land" a sparse piece (unresolved is the home; resolution is the growth he refused).
- Furniture-music pastiche as wallpaper (the pieces are short and exact; ambient length is not the point).
- The eccentric-genius story as branding (seven identical velvet suits, the umbrella, the Arcueil room: biography is not material).
