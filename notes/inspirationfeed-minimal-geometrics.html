Status

Visual study completed 2026-09-19 on 6 of the 50 pieces, plus the article text and Zitzmann's artist statement. The 50 images are all in the same visual language and construction vocabulary, so the sample is representative, but the remaining 44 were not viewed.

What it is

A daily-practice project: one minimal geometric composition per day, rendered digitally in vector software but presented with paper grain so each piece reads like a physical print. Zitzmann's own line: lines, curves, rectangles, circles, and triangles are both a simplification of the real world and its building blocks.

Core techniques (visible in the work)

  • Construction-geometry made into the artwork. Classic Euclidean

constructions (nested circles, tangent spirals, golden-section diagonals) are not the scaffolding, they are the finished piece. Thin single-stroke lines, usually one ink color per print.

  • Recursive nesting. Several pieces repeat one motif at decreasing

scale: a spiral of tangent circles along a horizontal axis, a dodecagon mandala built from overlapped copies of one polyline, a triangle spiral that reads like a golden-ratio nautilus.

  • Overlapping translucent fills. A pentagonal rosette built from five

overlapping translucent pentagons on near-black; the layering order produces the color variation, no palette switching needed.

  • Curved meridian fields. One standout: a circle over a square with

a family of sweeping curved lines, like longitudes on a tilted globe, drawn in two ink colors (blue foreground, pale gray background).

  • Two-tone diagonals. A pair of rectangles with muted gray/beige

fills overlaid with dark diagonal construction lines and a single thin circle; restraint doing the heavy lifting.

Palette and composition choices

  • Muted, desaturated inks: slate blue, olive gray, warm taupe, sage, on

paper-white or near-black grounds.

  • Everything centered or on a quiet grid; generous white space; no more

than two or three visual elements per piece.

  • The paper-grain texture is a deliberate choice: digital work dressed

to feel handmade, which softens the math.

What makes it sing

  • One idea per piece, executed completely. The best compositions are

diagrams that stopped being diagrams: the construction lines are the aesthetic.

  • The daily constraint did the creative work: a format this tight forces

variety out of almost nothing (just scale, rotation, overlap, nesting).

What is overdone (avoid-list)

  • The minimalist single-motif print can tip into wall-art sameness if

everything stays centered and pastel. Pieces like the meridian-circle one work because the lines have direction and tension, not just symmetry.

  • Beware dressing digital geometry in fake paper grain and calling it

analog: fine as a finish, weak as a concept.

Ideas for our own work

  • Construction-line aesthetics translate directly to code: pick one

construction (tangent-circle spiral, meridian field, pentagon rosette), generate it parametrically with a seed, and let the construction lines be the piece. A seeded "geometry daily" series would be a natural fit for the repo's daily-creation goal.

  • Overlap-transparency rosettes are cheap to compute and read as

sophisticated: n rotated copies of one polygon with alpha blending.

  • The two-ink trick (foreground line family in a saturated color,

background family in pale gray) gives instant depth for almost no extra code.
