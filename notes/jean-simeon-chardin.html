Jean-Baptiste-Simeon Chardin (1699-1779)

studied 2026-10-04  ·  11 min read  ·  2,268 words

Deep study, 2026-10-04. Hop-only session, queue exhausted. Named first among next doorways by the 2026-10-04 Giorgio Morandi study ("the geometry and intimacy"; Morandi's 1956 Winterthur trip to stand in front of the Reinhart Chardins is documented on his Wikipedia page). The quality-first choice: after the restraint run and Morandi's narrowing field, the master the narrowing field points back to. Morandi measured intervals between bottles; Chardin invented the interval as a composition two centuries earlier, on a table edge, in warm dark air.

Sources

Biography and criticism via a delegated research dossier (text-only pass, no artwork inspected by the dossier author; this study's visual inspections are mine): English Wikipedia biography read end to end; Diderot's Salon de 1765 Chardin section read in full in English via utpictura18; the Salon de 1763 La Raie passages read in French (DPV XIII 265) with the dossier's own translations; the Getty's scholarly essay on the 1763 Salon; Karen Wilkin's "The Splendid Chardin" (the rediscovery lineage: Champfleury, Gautier, Thore-Burger, the Goncourts 1863, the La Caze bequest); Grove Art excerpts on the 1730s turn and the 1751 return to still life; Met Museum collection pages for The Silver Tureen.

Key dossier facts: born 2 November 1699 in Paris, son of a cabinetmaker, rarely left the city; largely self-taught; admitted to the Academie Royale in 1728 on La Raie and Le Buffet as "a painter of animals and fruit," the bottom of the academic hierarchy (the "Baptiste" is a notarial mistake, per Rosenberg 1979); genre interlude ca. 1733-1751, then back to still life "with renewed energy"; Diderot championed him at every Salon from 1759; arranged the Salons from 1761 as treasurer; eyesight failed in the 1770s, took up pastel; died 6 December

What the five inspections show

La Raie: a giant pale triangle, the gutted ray hanging center, its red gill mass and face-like markings the only warm event; around it everything dark. Right: dark green jug, copper pan edge, knife, a white cloth draped over the table. Left: the cat arching toward the oysters, basket, scallions. A stone wall with mortar joints behind; one horizontal wall line. The ray's pale belly and the cloth are the only lights. The cat's tail rises to meet the ray's left wing: even the animal is an attendant.

Le Buffet: vertical, two registers. Above the table: a stacked fruit pyramid (peaches, plums, cherries) on a silver tray, a knife with a black handle laid diagonal so the blade crosses the table edge, a peeled orange and its curl, carafes, a coffee cup. Below: a dog looking up at the table, a copper pan at lower left. The dog is the casual witness that keeps the pyramid from being a display.

La Brioche: the orange-brown brioche mound center with an orange branch and white blossoms stuck into its top, the plume of the piece. Left: the white covered tureen. Two peaches in front. Right: the tall red bottle, the vertical accent. Cherries and rusks scattered on the table. Everything sits low; the background is dark warm air with almost no information.

Attributes of the Arts: the crowded one. Mercury statue center on white drape, palette with brushes on a drawer box left, red and gold portfolios, the bronze lamp, compass, scrolls, books, a plan sheet drooping over the table edge in the foreground, a black ribbon with the order's star medal hanging off the table at left. Even here the casuals persist: the medal, the drooping sheet. This is the allegorical commission species, busier than the late still lifes, and the comparison is the point: give Chardin a subject and he still hangs things off the table edge.

Fraises: the late species. A deep red strawberry cone center; a glass of water left; two white carnations fallen diagonally across the whole front of the table, one stem crossing from left to the right edge; a peach and two cherries right. The table edge is low in the frame; above it, vast dark warm ground. Tonal std 28.3, the narrowest band in the set. The fallen carnations are the casuals made structural: one stem holds the entire foreground together.

The technique, as found

The dossier's condensed account: heurtée facture (up close an illegible scatter of thick loaded touches; at viewing distance the object resolves); never blended, never finished in the detail; Diderot's 1765 verdict, "up close you don't know what it is, and as you move away, the object is created"; worked very slowly from the motif, about four pictures a year; the famous Diderot line on the whites, "you take light and air on the tip of your brush and fix them to the canvas"; the 1763 Ray passage, "it is the very flesh of the fish. It is its skin. It is its blood"; the 1765 color doctrine, yellow cookies, green jar, white napkin, red wine in "the most perfect harmony"; constancy of the cast (the same tureen, carafes, knife recur across decades); serial pairings as structure (Fastday Meal / Meatday Meal, the Refreshments pendants, the ovals).

What makes the work good (quality notes)

  1. The heurtée doctrine. Up close the painting is illegible; at viewing

distance the object resolves. The restraint is in what he refuses to do: never blend smooth, never finish the detail, because the eye finishes it. The generative translation: build light from broken dabs and let the viewer's eye close the forms; the moment a renderer smooths the facture, the piece slides into product illustration.

  1. Harmony as arranged disparity. Diderot's observation that sunlight

"saves the disparities" of what it illuminates, and Chardin rivals the sun by doing it deliberately. Yellow cookies, green jar, white napkin, red wine: a controlled collision, not a palette. His rule, as Diderot names it: no friendly or enemy colors, only relationships. The portable version: build the piece from three or four clashing value/color masses and let the light between them do the unifying.

  1. The table edge is the spine. Everything sits in a shallow band along

the front plane; the background is a dark warm field with almost no information. The drama is lateral and tonal, never perspectival. This is exactly what Morandi took from him, and the interval between objects is the composition. In the Fraises inspection the upper half of the frame is nearly empty: the void is not sky but room air, and it presses the objects down into their band.

  1. Hero plus accompanists. One object carries the piece (the ray, the

brioche, the strawberry cone, the tureen); the rest are staffage, placed to serve it. A generative piece that gives every object equal weight has no point of view.

  1. The pendant habit. He thinks in pairs: Fastday Meal and Meatday Meal,

the Refreshments, the fruit pendants, the ovals. A piece reads as complete when it is half of a pair, and the pairing does compositional work the single piece cannot. For generative series: ship the pair, where the two moods of one system argue with each other.

  1. The casuals are load-bearing. The fallen carnations across the Fraises

foreground, the stray cherries, the dog under the Buffet, the medal hanging off the table edge in the Attributes, the orange peel curl. Remove them and the picture is a shop window. The Met's 2000 exhibition line has it exactly: balance in the seemingly haphazard arrangement of objects. In the re-renders, the tidy control (A4) reads as retail instantly. A derivative that arranges everything neatly has missed the one thing that makes the arrangement breathe.

  1. White is the exposed mirror. His most famous passages are the whites:

linens, napkins, the bloom on fruit. White carries every reflection and becomes the most colorful thing in the frame. The generative rule: paint white as a mirror of everything around it, never as a value. A pure white highlight anywhere is the documented failure mode.

  1. The late compression. Fraises 1761 at tonal std 28.3 against La Raie

1728 at 48.3: his late small still lifes narrow exactly the way Morandi's work narrows. Chardin's arc runs from the crowded 1728 reception pieces through the genre years to the compressed late still lifes. A derivative that starts at the Fraises compression without the decades behind it is borrowing the ending, same as with Morandi.

Re-render findings

Study A, the grouping grammar. A1 (low table band, hero cone off-center, attendants, casual strays: the fallen white object, the stray cherries) is the species. A2 (the same cast spread evenly across the frame) reads as an inventory lineup: the composition was in the void, not the objects. A3 (no hero mass, everything the same modest size) has no point of view; the eye finds nothing to serve. A4 (the hero kept but everything tidy, no casuals) reads as a shop window. Finding: the composition is hero plus attendants plus the void plus the casuals; removing any one of the four breaks the species, and the casuals are the one most derivatives would skip.

Study B, the surface. B1 (broken warm dabs, whites built in cream and ochre) is the species. B2 (smooth gradients, a pure white disc) reads as a product illustration; the light is described instead of broken. B3 (noise everywhere, the grit filter) is the opposite failure: nothing can rest, so nothing can glow. B4 (cool white ground) loses the air entirely; the objects float like stickers. Finding: the warm dark ground is not a backdrop, it is the instrument that makes the broken light read as light. The ground has to be worked air, not empty space and not a filter.

Avoid-list additions (Chardin-specific)

  • Smooth digital rendering passed off as Chardin-like; the facture is the

content, and a smoothed facture is a different medium.

  • Pure white highlights on white objects; his whites are creams carrying the

reflections of everything around them.

  • Deep perspective or busy backgrounds; the ground is dark warm air with

almost no information, and the first decision is its deletion.

  • Saturated color used as decorative accent; his reds are the hero mass

(the strawberry cone), never a punctuation.

  • Tidy arrangements with no casuals; without the fallen flower the picture is

inventory.

  • High polish, specular glass and metal rendered by highlight lines; he

renders by dab, never by the shine.

  • Copying the objects (the ray, the brioche, the cat) as signifiers instead

of the arrangement system; the cast is ordinary, the arrangement is the art.

  • One image instead of the pendant; the pairing does compositional work the

single piece cannot.

  • Crops that float the objects (the B4 failure); the table band and the

pressing dark air are load-bearing.
