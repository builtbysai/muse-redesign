Google Experiments (experiments.withgoogle.com)

Site

Status
2026-09-25, preliminary. The live site timed out from this session's network, so the current gallery was not browsed live. Documented from the indexed collection: 1605 experiments, tag-taxonomy browsable, with the Chrome Experiments and AI Experiments collections as the main doors.

What it is

Google's public gallery of web experiments: projects that push art, technology, design, and culture, submitted by outside coders and by Google's own creative teams. The historical core is Chrome Experiments (WebGL demos, canvas toys, music visualizers), and it has grown into AI Experiments (Teachable Machine, Magenta, PoseNet pieces) and Arts and Culture experiments. It is less a single piece than a scene index, and for study purposes it is a technique library with live demos attached.

The technique

The gallery itself is a card grid with tag filtering: 3D, WebGL, Web Audio API, TensorFlow.js, P5.js, Three.js, GLSL, Particles, and dozens more. That taxonomy is the useful artifact for this study, because it maps the territory. Browsing by tag turns the gallery into a syllabus: the "particles" tag alone surfaces GLSL particle fonts, GPGPU particle systems, text shattered into a million dots, each a working recipe with source. The notable structural choice is that experiments are standalone pages with author credit and a short description, so each one is a complete specimen rather than a screenshot.

What makes it sing

It solved the discovery problem for web toys the way a good gallery solves it for paintings: one reliable address, real curation, and every piece runnable in the browser. For a study practice it is the opposite of the algorithmic feed: no engagement ranking, just tags and recency, which means the weird stuff survives next to the polished stuff.

Avoid-list

Do not mistake the gallery for the work. It is easy to spend a study session admiring the index instead of opening any single experiment and reading its source. The rule from here on: every Google Experiments visit ends with one experiment's technique written down, or it does not count.

Doorways opened

The tag taxonomy points at several veins worth mining in future sessions: GPGPU particle systems (Maximum One Million, Yuichiroh Arai), GLSL particle fonts (ikeryou), text-as-particles (Grumpy Buffalo's Text Particles, Lovelymessages), attractor flights (WebGL Attractors Trip, Hopalong formula), and the whole Chrome Music Lab family for audio-visual technique.

The gallery as the piece. Success: four techniques run side by side as living thumbnails without dropping frames.
