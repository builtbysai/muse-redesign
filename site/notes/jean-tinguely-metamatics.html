The Metamatics (1954-1959)

Small junk-built machines that make abstract drawings. A felt pen or pencil is clamped onto a moving mount and strikes a sheet of paper that is itself on a moving mount; the drawing is the relative motion of two moving things, produced amid the machine's clattering. At the 1959 Paris Biennale visitors turned cranks while gears spun in jerky motions and the pen arm made staccato slashes on the paper. One machine reportedly produced over 40,000 original works during a Biennale. Tristan Tzara, present at the 1959 rue de Beaux Arts showing, exclaimed: "This is finally the culmination of the Dada movement."

Mechanical disorder as design goal

The Hulten quote is the whole engineering spec, inverted: "In machines intended for practical use the engineer tries to reduce the irregularities as much as possible. Tinguely is after that exact opposite. His objective is mechanical disorder. His cogwheels are so constructed that they jump the cogs continually jam, and start turning again, unpredictably... The same movement can appear ten times in succession and then, apparently, never be repeated again."

The Tate pack lists the actual operator variables that keep no two works alike: the ink, paint or pen fixed in place; the fluidity, color or density of the drawing material; how close to the paper the implement is positioned; whether the user changes any of these factors during operation; and how long the machine is allowed to run. The uniqueness is not a random seed. It is the sum of material choices, machine state, and run time. This is worth sitting with: his variety guarantee is physical and procedural, not numerical.

The coin slot

The oral history is frank about it: at first you had to put money in to make the machines work. Tinguely: "This was not fun. This was not fun, too. This was a real mechanism, a technological mechanism to bring the people to go into it." De Saint Phalle glosses it as engagement: people who paid engage themselves. The transaction is participation design, not revenue: a small cost that turns a spectator into an operator, and every operator into a co-author. The Garcia history frames the result as "collaborative" art machines that combine user and machine into one artist, "completely cutting Tinguely out of the process." The artist designs the conditions of authorship, then is absent from the output.

Allowing the machine independence

Tate's biography line: from the beginning he "always allowed the machine a certain independence from its creator, hoping that it would develop a character or even a 'mind' of its own." His own summary: "The machine is an instrument that permits me to be poetic. If you respect the machine, if you enter into a game with the machine, then perhaps you can make a truly joyous machine, by joyous I mean free." Compare Lehni's Frankenstein moment and his "the idea visits other people." The lineage is consistent across seventy years: the good machines have character, and character arrives through imperfection, not spec.

The drawings, seen

The genuine Metamatic drawing (thin graphite on aged paper) is calmer than the legend suggests. What it actually shows:

  • Long single-stroke traversals that sweep the full sheet, then reverse

direction suddenly and return, making looping hairpin returns.

  • Sudden angular kinks mid-arc: the pen changes direction without

stopping, the signature of a gear jumping its cogs.

  • Dwell points: small hatched or scribbled circles where the pen sat and

worked one spot, graphite accumulating. These read as punctuation, not mistakes.

  • Staccato: the line has a struck quality, short bursts rather than one

continuous glide. The pen touches down, slashes, lifts.

  • Thin, dry line throughout. No pressure modeling, no wet ink pooling.

The drama is in the path, not the stroke weight.

The two Tinguely hand lithographs are a different animal: dense, colored (blue, red, ochre, black), hand-annotated with numbers and his signature. These are drawings OF machines, compositional studies with schematic energy, and they are useful precisely as contrast: when the human hand draws a machine, it draws loops, belts, and counterweights too. The machine drawings and the drawings of machines share a graphic vocabulary, and that is not an accident.

Local re-render: two mounts, one relative path

I built the mechanism from the descriptions, not from any Metamatic blueprint: a two-link pen arm driven at two incommensurate angular speeds, plus discrete "jam" events (one link's velocity abruptly scaled with a phase kick, decaying back to nominal as the belt re-seats), plus a paper mount that rocks and breathes beneath the pen (bounded oscillation, so the relative motion wanders the sheet instead of sliding off it). The pen draws in staccato bursts, lifts, relocates; slow tip motion accumulates graphite into dwell blobs.

Two iterations. The first used a one-way paper slide and everything drifted off the right edge: wrong, because a real paper mount is bounded. The second (oscillating paper frame) fills the page with long looping traversals, jam kinks, loop-returns, and dwell knots. It reads as the same family as the real drawing. The honest differences: my arcs are a little too regular between jams, and my page fills more densely in the center than the real one. The lesson held anyway: the look of the work comes from the RELATIVE motion of two imperfect mechanisms, not from the arm alone. Compose the difference, not the part.

Technique takeaways

  • Design the failure modes as the palette. The Metamatics are not random

number generators with pen arms. The variety comes from constructed irregularity: gears that jump, belts that re-seat, paper that shifts. The randomness has a mechanical signature, which is why no two drawings are the same AND every drawing is recognizably a Metamatic.

  • Relative motion is the drawing. Two moving mounts meeting is a richer

generator than one pen path. Any system where the mark lands on a moving surface composes in the difference of two motions.

  • The uniqueness guarantee is physical. Operator choices (material,

distance, duration) plus machine state replace the seed. For gallery pieces, "no two alike" built from physics reads differently than "no two alike" built from a hash.

  • The coin slot is participation design. A tiny real cost converts a

viewer into an operator and an operator into a co-author. The transaction is the interaction design, not the business model.

  • Author the conditions, not the output. Tinguely's cut from the process

is deliberate: the artwork is the machine plus the rules of engagement. Everything after is someone else's drawing.

  • Character over correctness. The Hulten quote and the Tate "mind of its

own" line say the same thing Lehni said about Hektor's Frankenstein moment: the good machine is the one whose imperfections you can name.

Avoid-list additions

  • Do not fake Dada with a noise function. A Perlin walk with kinks is not

a Metamatic. The disorder must have a mechanical signature (specific failure modes, material, dwell) or it reads as texture, not character.

  • Do not copy the scribble look without the two-mount mechanism. The

hairpin loops and staccato slashes come from relative motion and pen strikes; imitating the marks with a single path misses the point.

  • Do not treat the audience input as decoration. The operator's choices

are half the generator. A piece that invites participation but changes nothing is a vending machine selling the same candy.

Honest gaps

  • No live Metamatic run seen in this session (museums, not YouTube, hold

the working ones; performance videos unwatched).

  • The auction-site scans are secondary reproductions; paper tone and

graphite texture are inferred from compressed JPEGs.

  • Tinguely's own hand lithographs inspected here are machine studies, not

machine outputs; the distinction is kept explicit in the notes.

  • The re-render is a procedural analogue of described principles, not a

reconstruction of any specific Metamatic's gearing.

Doorways for next sessions

  • Akira Kanayama (Gutai, 1957): electronic toy car painting on canvas,

via Garcia's history. Same year as the Metamatics, the electronic fork of machine painting: "removal of the artist's intentions" with motors instead of gears. Compare mechanical disorder versus electronic automation as the source of surprise.

  • Roman Verostko and the algorists: the pen-plotter pole of the drawing

machine lineage, after Vera Molnar (studied) and before Lehni. Hodos drawings, code as score.

  • Le Cyclograveur and the Metamatic parade of 13 May 1960 (French

pedagogical PDF): machines as street procession, the machine as public actor rather than gallery device.

  • Niki de Saint Phalle: the oral history makes her a co-witness; her

shooting paintings are the violent, audience-triggered sibling of the drawing machines.

  • Homage to New York (1960): the self-destroying machine, MoMA. The

Metamatic logic pushed to its limit: a machine whose only output is its own destruction.
